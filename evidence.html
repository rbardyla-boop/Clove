EVIDENCE FIRST · VISIBLE LIMITS · CORRECTION FRIENDLY

Show me what would prove you wrong.

CloveLearn is built around a simple rule: a claim should be inspectable before it becomes part of somebody's worldview. Sources matter. Counterevidence matters. Uncertainty stays visible.

Claim status

ESTABLISHED — supported by strong, converging evidence appropriate to the claim.

PROBABLE — the evidence leans one way, but important uncertainty or limitations remain.

HYPOTHESIS — plausible and testable, but not established.

PERSONAL EXPERIENCE — a lived observation. Real as an experience; not automatically a population-level fact.

UNSUPPORTED — we do not currently have enough evidence to responsibly make the claim.

CUT — the claim did not survive review and should not be used.

The receipt

When a factual claim matters, the useful unit is not a confident paragraph. It is a receipt that another person can inspect.

CLAIM — what exactly is being asserted?

STATUS — established, probable, hypothesis, personal experience, unsupported or cut?

SOURCE — what is the strongest source we can actually point to?

COUNTEREVIDENCE — what serious evidence pushes the other way?

WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND — what observation, result or source would force a revision?

Primary sources first when they matter

For laws, official statistics, government actions, historical records and quoted statements, CloveLearn tries to reach the underlying statute, table, report, transcript, recording, court decision or archived document rather than stopping at somebody else's summary.

Current-web absence is not always evidence of absence

Pages disappear. URLs change. Documents are replaced. When deletion or scrubbing is plausible, the search is not finished because a current page returns 404. Archived pages, old URL paths, cached copies, PDFs, mirrors and contemporaneous references can matter.

Person over category

Group-level evidence can be useful. It does not get to sentence an individual. A population average is a clue about a population, not a verdict about the person standing in front of you.

The rule behind all of it

Reality gets the last vote.

If a favourite theory loses to the evidence, the theory loses. The point is not to build a prettier certainty machine. The point is to become harder to bullshit — including by ourselves.
