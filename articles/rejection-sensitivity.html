RSD and ADHD — The Field Manual

Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria: The Alarm That Can't Tell the Difference

By Ryan Bardyla · Canadian Army Veteran · From The Biological Bruise

The Alarm

I remember sitting in an exam room after getting a perfect score on a test that I had worked for months to prepare for. I folded the paper the moment I saw the grade. Not because I was embarrassed. Because I knew what perfect scores mean: they expect perfect next time. And next time I might not deliver.

The alarm didn't fire after the grade. The alarm fired before the examiner finished handing back the papers. Before I had consciously processed the situation. Before I could think. The physiological response — the flood of shame, the shrinking — arrived at the same time as the information. They weren't sequential. They were simultaneous.

That's what RSD is. Not oversensitivity. Not drama. Not character weakness. It's an alarm system that fires in under 200 milliseconds — faster than conscious thought — and produces a pain response that has been measured, in neuroimaging studies, at intensities comparable to physical pain. The alarm and the response arrive together, before the prefrontal cortex has been notified there's a situation to assess.

Hardware, Not Character

Here is what happens in a neurotypical brain when a rejection signal arrives:

Neurotypical Pathway
Rejection signal received
Amygdala assesses threat level
PFC receives signal and modulates
Proportional emotional response
Processing. Recovery. Move on.
ADHD + RSD Pathway
Rejection signal received
Amygdala assesses: THREAT
PFC cannot modulate — first casualty
Full unmodulated alarm fires
Pain at physical-injury intensity. Loop.

The critical difference is step three. In a neurotypical brain, the prefrontal cortex receives the signal and applies context, nuance, and proportionality. It says: this is a critical email, not a predator. Respond accordingly. In the ADHD brain with RSD, the PFC doesn't get to weigh in. The alarm has already fired and flooded the system. By the time conscious awareness arrives, the physiological response is already running.

People tell RSD sufferers to "just try harder to catch it" or "notice when you're spiraling." These instructions require the prefrontal cortex. The prefrontal cortex is the first casualty. You can't use the faculty that's offline to compensate for the fact that it's offline.

RSD vs Narcissistic Injury

These two get conflated constantly, including by clinicians who should know better. The distinction matters because the interventions are different.

Narcissistic injury is about protecting a self-image. The wound happens when the external reality threatens the constructed narrative of who you are. The response is often defensive aggression or blame-shifting — the self-image needs to be defended, so the threat to it gets attacked. Recovery is slow because the narrative has to be rebuilt.

RSD is an alarm. It is not protecting a story — it is running a threat assessment. The response is not aggression at the source; it is pain at the self. Shame, withdrawal, collapse. And the key clinical marker: rapid recovery once the alarm subsides. People with RSD can, often within hours, articulate clearly that the alarm misfired. They can identify the misfire after the fact with complete lucidity. The problem was never insight. The problem was the alarm arrives before insight has a chance to run.

If you find yourself recovering quickly and being able to say, clearly, "that was RSD, that wasn't real" — that's diagnostic. Narcissistic injury doesn't recover that way. It defends.

The Contact Protocol — 30 Seconds

You cannot prevent the alarm. The alarm fires at sub-200ms. What you can create is a 30-second gap between the alarm and the reaction. That gap is the intervention.

Seconds 0–10
Name It
Say it — out loud or internally — without deliberating: "The alarm is firing. This is RSD. This is not the truth." You are not arguing with the alarm. You are labeling the source. Labeling reduces amygdala activation. You're not trying to feel better. You're trying to create the gap.
Seconds 10–20
Breathe — 4:6 Ratio
Inhale for 4 counts. Exhale for 6. The extended exhale activates the parasympathetic nervous system and begins reducing cortisol. This is not about calming down. It's about giving the PFC the 10 seconds it needs to start coming back online. The breathing is the protocol, not the metaphor.
Seconds 20–30
Locate the Alarm
Where is it in your body? Chest, throat, gut, head? This is not metaphor work — it's a grounding mechanism that forces present-tense sensory processing. The amygdala pulls attention into past evidence and future catastrophe. Locating the alarm in the body returns attention to the present moment, where the actual threat level is assessable.

The protocol doesn't eliminate the episode. The pain is still real. The alarm is still firing. What the protocol creates is 30 seconds between the alarm and the reaction. That window is what prevents the second event: the shame spiral.

The Shame Spiral

Most of the damage from RSD doesn't happen during the episode. It happens in the loop that follows.

The alarm fires. You react — send the text you shouldn't have sent, withdraw from the situation, say something defensive, go silent. Then comes the awareness of what you just did. And then — because RSD is a rejection-sensitivity alarm — you feel the alarm fire again on your own reaction. You are now rejecting yourself for how you responded to being rejected. The alarm uses itself as fuel.

The shame spiral is the alarm running in a closed loop. Each pass through intensifies the next because each pass adds more evidence to the 3AM brief.

The protocol interrupts the loop at step two — between the alarm and the reaction — not at step one. You cannot prevent step one. The alarm fires. But if the reaction doesn't happen, the spiral has no fuel. The 30 seconds are not about managing the emotion. They're about creating a gap before the reaction that would, in turn, generate the shame that would trigger the loop.

You ran the protocol. The alarm fired. You didn't react. The spiral didn't start. The episode passes faster than it would have if you'd let it loop.

That is the mechanism. Not a cure. Not a fix. A gap. And inside the gap, enough space to not make it worse.
