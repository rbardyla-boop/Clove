The Attraction Protocol: Three Levers That Build the Signal

By Ryan Bardyla · Canadian Army Veteran · From Ride the Unicorn

Stop Looking for the Script

If what you're looking for is the right words, the right sequence of moves, the specific thing that unlocks the specific person you're thinking about — this protocol can't give you that. Not because it's being withheld. Because it doesn't exist.

The nervous system in the person across from you detects performance before language has a chance to intervene. The autonomic scan runs before you open your mouth. The limbic system has already rendered a preliminary verdict on what you're broadcasting by the time you've said hello. If you're performing, they know. They don't know they know. They just know something is off. And that mismatch is signal death.

What this protocol gives you is the architecture. Three levers that, when built and running simultaneously, produce the signal that Brain One, Brain Two, Brain Three, and the autonomic nervous system are all reading for. Not as a manufactured output. As the natural consequence of a man who has built something real and shows up as someone who is genuinely somewhere.

"The architecture is not complicated to understand. Executing it is not easy. Those are different problems with different solutions — and conflating them is what has produced most of the useless advice in the self-development space for the last thirty years."
LEVER 01 Mission
Direction over position · Momentum over achievement

Helen Fisher's neuroimaging research is precise: men with clear, genuine mission generate a measurably stronger attraction response — across cultures, across age groups, across stated preference categories — independent of income, physical appearance, or social status. Not the outcome of the mission. Not the achievement already banked. The mission itself. The direction. The momentum.

David Buss's cross-cultural data confirms the mechanism: women select for resource acquisition capacity — not resources already held, but the trajectory toward them. The man in motion generates the same selection signal as the man who has arrived. Direction over position. Momentum over achievement. The man building something is often more compelling than the man who finished building it years ago and has been administering it since.

The mission needs three things to generate the signal at the level the dopamine system requires:

  • C1 Specific Completion State. The mission must be specific enough that you know, in binary terms, whether you've done it. "I'm working on my health" has no completion state, no failure mode, no activation. "I'm completing a 5K under 25 minutes by November 1st" has a completion state. The specificity is not pedantic — the dopamine system does not fire on vague direction. It fires on the identifiable difference between success and failure at a defined objective.
  • C2 Genuine Consequence. Something real has to be at stake. Not manufactured urgency — "I'll feel bad about myself if I don't" — which the dopamine system correctly identifies as low-grade threat rather than real stakes. Real stakes: financial, professional, physical, relational. Real stakes create the consequence architecture that makes the signal genuine rather than performed.
  • C3 Sustained Meaning Past the Novelty Window. The dopamine system normalizes every environment. The exciting becomes ordinary. The mission that was generating strong signal on day one will generate declining signal by day sixty unless there is a layer beneath the novelty that remains relevant when the novelty is gone. That layer is meaning — the connection between the mission and something that matters at a depth below the activation of the dopamine spike.

The attraction implication: when you are genuinely occupied by a real mission, you are naturally unavailable in the ways the dopamine attraction system requires. Not performing unavailability as a tactic — actually occupied in a way that makes total availability structurally impossible. She can feel the difference between performed scarcity and genuine occupation at the level of the nervous system.

The man whose primary cognitive activity is managing her response has no signal to generate. His attention is consumed by the monitoring process. The monitoring process is the performance. The performance is the signal death. Build the mission. The occupation generates the signal as a byproduct.

LEVER 02 Presence
Ventral vagal state · You cannot fake it — only build it

Stephen Porges' Polyvagal Theory provides the mechanism. The ventral vagal state — genuine calm regulation, the neurological condition of a man who is actually okay in this moment regardless of external conditions — generates a measurable signal. Not a metaphorical signal. A physiological one.

The autonomic nervous system in the person across from you is conducting a continuous assessment of the regulatory state of the people in its environment. A man performing confidence while anxious underneath — whose stated calm is not matching his physiological state — broadcasts a frequency the nervous system reads as mismatch. Something is off. The mismatch registers as low-level threat. The attraction response does not activate under mismatch.

James Pennebaker's research on emotional suppression documents the cost precisely: subjects instructed to suppress visible emotional expression showed measurably elevated cardiovascular response. The suppression is labor. The labor has a physiological cost. The cost is visible in the body. The nervous system in the person across from you detects the cost. You cannot suppress your way to ventral vagal.

Build the State — Three Protocols
Pre-load before high-stakes interactions. Thirty minutes of sustained physical output before any interaction where presence matters. The transient hypofrontality produced by sustained physical demand reduces the prefrontal cortex's activity, which reduces the emotional reactivity it was failing to modulate. You arrive with a different neurological baseline — calmer, less reactive, more genuinely regulated rather than performing regulation.

Decision elimination before the interaction. The analytical brain burns executive function on unstructured decisions — what to wear, which route, how to open the conversation. Every unstructured decision draws from the same reserve that emotional regulation requires. Pre-decide every variable that can be pre-decided. Build the capsule wardrobe. Lock the route. Arrive with resources intact.

Single-channel focus during the interaction. Most men in socially significant interactions run two simultaneous processes: engaging with the person in front of them and monitoring how the engagement is landing. The monitoring process — tracking whether you're doing well, whether the last thing you said landed — is the RSD running preemptive evaluation. Drop the monitor. Redirect all available attention to the single channel: what is actually happening in this interaction right now? Not "how am I doing" — what is actually happening. The difference between those two questions is the difference between genuine presence and the performance of it.

When you catch the monitor running — and you will catch it, frequently, especially in the early weeks — name it internally. "Monitor active." Then return to the single channel: what is actually happening right now? This is the protocol. Run it until it becomes the default.

LEVER 03 Calibrated Edge
Non-reactivity · Investment without contingency · Hold when things buck

The bad boy generates attraction in Phase I and Phase II environments not primarily because he is cruel or dominant — but because he reads as regulated. His internal state does not visibly depend on whether she approves of him. He is not monitoring. He is not contingent. The jerk runs the same surface pattern from chaos underneath. The operator runs the same surface pattern from actual regulation underneath. The nervous system in the person across from him reads the difference. Not always immediately. Over time.

  • → Non-reactivity under genuine pressure. Not stoicism performed — actual regulation maintained because the physical protocol and mission clarity are doing the jobs they're supposed to do. Boyd's OODA loop applied to interpersonal dynamics: Observe what's actually happening. Orient based on the architecture. Decide the chosen response. Act from the oriented position rather than the reactive one. The man who closes his OODA loop faster than the situation is escalating holds frame under real pressure.
  • → Investment without contingency. Non-neediness is not coldness. It is not indifference. It is the natural output of a man who has a real life — a mission that claims his attention, a physical architecture that regulates his nervous system — so that her response is an addition to something already complete rather than the primary input his functioning depends on. When contingency fires — when you catch yourself tracking her response rather than moving toward the mission — the redirect is the mission. Do this enough times and the mission becomes the primary channel.
  • → Hold when things buck. Gottman's research: the variable separating couples who survive conflict from those who don't is not the absence of conflict. It's the ratio. Five positive interactions to every negative one, maintained over time, builds a foundation with enough reserve to absorb the cost of difficult moments. You build the ratio before the crisis. The crisis tests what was built before it arrived. Show up for the ratio in ordinary time — genuine presence in the routine interactions — and you have something to hold onto when the unremarkable days stop.

What Kills All Three Levers

PERFORMANCE
Doing the right things for the wrong reasons, with performance as the primary orientation. The nervous system across from you detects this before the conversation starts. Stop performing. Build the actual conditions. The signal is produced by a man who is genuinely occupied, actually present, and running toward something real.
CONTINGENCY
Your emotional state tracking her response moment to moment. The instant her response owns your day, the non-neediness signal dies. Contingent attention is not genuine attention — it is a form of monitoring that looks like care and functions like absence. Return to the mission every time the contingency fires. Not as discipline. As the operational redirect that builds the internal state over time.
STONEWALLING
Present coordinates, absent person. Physically there, actually elsewhere. This destroys Lever Two completely. If you are in the room, be in the room. If you cannot be there genuinely, do not be there. There is no middle ground — the ghost in the house produces measurable relational damage that compounds silently.

The Three-Week Execution Protocol

The honest projection: three weeks does not produce a transformed man. It produces a man with accurate intelligence on his current status across three levers and a daily architecture that has begun feeding the signal rather than draining it. The destination is built over months and years. The value of the three weeks is specifically this: at the end, you know whether you are actually doing the work or performing the idea of doing it.

WEEK ONE
Mission Architecture
Day 1–2: Name the mission. Write it down with the specific completion state, the consequence clock, and the face it fails. If you can't complete this in fifteen minutes, the mission doesn't exist in a form the dopamine system can fire on. Day 3–5: Identify three decisions currently burning executive function that can be pre-decided. Meal structure, morning sequence, weekly schedule. Eliminate those decisions this week. Day 6–7: Name the accountability partner and brief them. One person. Real consequence. Friday completion report.
WEEK TWO
Physical Architecture
Day 8–10: Lock the morning protocol. Fixed wake time. Physical output before device engagement or social contact. Minimum thirty minutes sustained output. Non-negotiable in the same category as medication — because for this hardware, it is medication. Day 11–12: Identify the spike management tool. Sprint, cold exposure, heavy resistance — whatever produces transient hypofrontality for your specific body. Test it in a low-stakes situation. Day 13–14: Run single-channel practice in three social interactions. Don't grade the interactions. Count how many times you catch the monitoring process running.
WEEK THREE
Integration
Day 15–17: Pre-load one high-stakes social interaction with physical output thirty minutes beforehand. Observe the difference in your regulatory baseline — not the performance difference, the internal state difference. Day 18–19: Identify one place where investment has become contingent. Name it without judgment. Apply the mission redirect. Day 20–21: Run the Status Stack Audit. Rate the current state of each lever 1–10. Mission clarity and occupation. Physical architecture and consistency. Social proof as natural consequence of the first two. Identify the lowest-rated lever. That's the constraint. The next month addresses the constraint exclusively.

The Morning Protocol

The morning is where the regulatory baseline for the day is set. The man who wakes to the phone — immediately entering the social evaluation environment, reading notifications, checking responses, processing other people's assessments before his own nervous system has come fully online — is setting his regulatory baseline in reactive mode. The day runs on that foundation.

The man who wakes and moves before anything else — who subjects his body to real physical demand before his nervous system has access to social media, notifications, or any external evaluation input — is setting his regulatory baseline in operational mode. The day runs on that foundation.

Fixed wake time. Physical output first. No device contact until the physical output is complete. Thirty minutes minimum. Outside if possible — the light exposure affects circadian rhythm, the environmental context affects the quality of the transient hypofrontality the exercise produces.

Three Protocol Questions
→ Did I move toward the mission today?
→ Was I actually present in the interactions that mattered — or was the monitor running?
→ Did I choose my responses, or react to conditions?
