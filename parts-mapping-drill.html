🗺 PARTS MAPPING

STEP 1 OF 5
IDENTIFY THE PART
Who just showed up?
Something is activated right now. A protector, a pattern, a voice that takes over. You don't need the clinical term. You need the name that fits. What do you call this part of yourself when it's running the show?
Examples:
"The Perfectionist" — the one who won't let me ship anything imperfect.
"The Rage" — the one who goes from 0 to 100 before I can think.
"The Ghost" — the one who checks out and goes numb.
"The Court Jester" — the one who makes everything a joke so nobody gets close.

STEP 2 OF 5
MAP THE PATTERN
What does this part actually do?
Not what it says — what it does. The observable behavior. When this part takes over, what happens to your voice, your body, your decisions? Be specific. This is reconnaissance, not judgment.
Examples:
"It makes me rehearse conversations for hours and rewrite emails six times before sending."
"It makes me go completely silent and leave the room. My wife calls it 'the shutdown.'"
"It scrolls until 3 AM so I don't have to feel whatever I'm avoiding."

STEP 3 OF 5
UNDERSTAND THE JOB
What is this part protecting you from?
Every part has a job. Even the destructive ones. Especially the destructive ones. The Perfectionist protects you from criticism. The Rage protects you from vulnerability. The Ghost protects you from pain you couldn't handle at the time. What does this part believe will happen if it stops?
IFS PRINCIPLE
There are no bad parts.
Every part started as a solution to a real problem. It may be outdated. It may be causing damage now. But it was never malicious. Understanding its original job is the first step toward asking it to step back — not by force, but by trust.

STEP 4 OF 5
FIND THE EXILE
What is underneath?
Behind every protector is an Exile — a younger, vulnerable part carrying the original pain. The Perfectionist guards a child who was told he wasn't good enough. The Rage guards a boy who learned that showing hurt got him hit. You don't need to go deep into the memory. Just name what's down there.
Examples:
"Underneath the anger is a kid who was terrified of his father and learned that being loud was safer than being small."
"Underneath the numbness is a version of me that was too overwhelmed to process what happened, so he just... left."
"Underneath the perfectionism is a boy who got love when he performed and silence when he didn't."
0 / 600
CAUTION
Don't force the door.
If the Exile's pain feels too big for right now, that's your system telling you something. Write what you can. This is not an endurance test. You can come back. The Exile isn't going anywhere — it's been waiting this long.
STEP 5 OF 5
MESSAGE FROM SELF
Write to this part.
Not from your head. From Self — the calm, clear center underneath all the parts. Not judgment. Not instructions. Not "you need to stop." Understanding. What would you say to this part if you could speak to it directly, with no agenda except compassion?
THE SELF IN IFS
Calm. Curious. Compassionate. Connected.
In IFS, Self is not a part. It is the core of who you are underneath every protector and every exile. You access it by asking: "Am I feeling curious toward this part, or critical?" If critical — that is another part. Step back until the curiosity arrives.
