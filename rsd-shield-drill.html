STEP 1 OF 6
THE TRIGGER
What just happened?
Describe the specific moment that set it off. Not the story your brain built around it — the actual event. A text that wasn't returned. A tone of voice. A look. A meeting where you were passed over. Be precise. The more specific you are, the easier it is to separate fact from feeling in the next steps.
Examples: "My boss gave feedback on my report in front of the team." / "A friend cancelled plans last minute." / "I saw a group chat I wasn't included in." / "My partner sighed when I started talking."

STEP 2 OF 6
THE STORY
What did your brain tell you it meant?
RSD doesn't just register rejection — it writes a screenplay. Within seconds your brain produced a full narrative about what that event means about you, about them, about everything. Write it down. The ugliest version. The one you'd never say out loud. That's the one running the show right now.
Examples: "They all think I'm incompetent." / "She doesn't actually want to be my friend." / "I'm the one everyone tolerates but nobody wants." / "I'll never be enough." / "They're going to leave."

STEP 3 OF 6
INTENSITY CHECK
How hard did it hit?
RSD hits fast and intense — 0 to 100 in seconds. Select the emotions you're feeling right now and rate their intensity. This isn't about judging the emotions. It's about measuring the blast radius so you can see the shift after the drill.
STEP 4 OF 6
FACT vs FEELING
What's real? What's the RSD talking?
Your nervous system produced a conviction. Now put it on trial. On the left: actual verifiable facts about what happened. On the right: the feelings and interpretations your brain added. One side is evidence. The other is the story. Fill at least one side.

STEP 5 OF 6
THE RSD SIGNATURE
Which patterns are running?
RSD has a fingerprint. It uses the same moves every time. Recognizing the pattern doesn't make the pain fake — it makes it identifiable. And identifiable means interruptible. Select all that showed up this time.
STEP 6 OF 6
THE CALIBRATED READ
What's the balanced version?
Not "everything is fine." Not "I'm overreacting." A version that accounts for the facts, acknowledges the pain, and doesn't let the RSD write the conclusion. What would you tell someone you respect if they described this exact situation to you?
Not this: "I'm sure they love me and everything's great."
This: "My boss gave direct feedback. That felt like an attack because of how my brain is wired. The feedback itself was about the report, not about me."
Or: "She cancelled. That hurt. It doesn't mean she doesn't care — it means plans changed."
Or: "I wasn't in the group chat. That stings. But I have evidence these people care about me from [specific example]."
