LOCAL FIRST · NO TELEMETRY · HUMAN FINAL DECISION

Write now. Go offline. Let the boring machinery help.

Clove Relay is a small publishing assistant built around one idea: automation should remove repetitive digital labour without taking over the judgment that made the work worth publishing.

Current status

Preparation path: qualified on a real Linux + Brave + Substack account.

Human-final-Schedule mode: implemented; final local smoke test and season freeze still pending.

Fully automated final-click scheduling: experimental and not required.

What Relay does

Relay validates an already-approved local source packet, opens the writer's authenticated publishing dashboard, creates an article, inserts the title/subtitle/body, checks that the content survived the editor, reaches publishing settings, and writes local receipts.

What Relay deliberately leaves to the human

The final publication decision. In the stable v0.1 design, the human inspects the visible page and performs the final Schedule click.

The machine handles the bullshit. The human keeps the decision.

Why not automate the last click?

Because the point is not to win a browser-automation contest. Publisher interfaces change. A tool that guesses confidently at the wrong button is worse than a tool that asks for a human when the state is ambiguous.

Relay is fail-closed: when it cannot prove the next action is safe, it stops or hands the control back to the person looking at the screen.

Security boundary

Relay does not ask for a publishing password, does not run a cloud account, does not collect telemetry, does not scrape subscriber data, does not bypass CAPTCHA, and does not silently fall back to immediate publishing.

What real testing found

The useful bugs were not theoretical. Linux browser process names, keyring-backed login state, duplicate selector aliases and a rerendering editor all broke early assumptions. The tool was changed to match what the real system did rather than what the code hoped it did.

Stable operating sequence

validate — check the source packet.

dry-run — prepare one article and stop before Schedule.

prepare — prepare one article; human clicks Schedule; Relay verifies.

prepare-batch — repeat sequentially; every final Schedule click stays human-owned.

Source

The current implementation lives in the public Clove repository under relay/. It is free and does not require AI to run.

The larger point

We keep using automation to remove the parts of life that build judgment while leaving people stuck doing the administrative garbage. Relay tries the opposite approach.

Keep the thinking. Keep the judgment. Keep the writing. Keep the decision. Automate the fucking dropdown menu.
