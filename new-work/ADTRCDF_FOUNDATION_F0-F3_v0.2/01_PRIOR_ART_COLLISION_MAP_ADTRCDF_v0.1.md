# F1 — Prior-Art Collision Map

**Working name:** ADTRCDF  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Audit date:** 2026-08-10  
**Scope:** Direct and adjacent prior art relevant to AI-assisted cross-domain scientific theory formation, hypothesis generation, knowledge synthesis, analogy, agentic science, validation, and benchmarking.

## Executive verdict

**Verdict: FRAMEWORK_PASS — NEW-FIELD CLAIM NOT CLEARED.**

The audit finds substantial prior art for nearly every individual ingredient originally proposed for ADTRCDF:

- disconnected-literature discovery;
- cross-domain knowledge discovery;
- structured scientific knowledge graphs;
- multi-agent scientific ideation;
- literature-grounded theory synthesis;
- shared-relational-structure analogy across domains;
- end-to-end AI scientist loops;
- laboratory-in-the-loop validation;
- interdisciplinary LLM benchmarks;
- cross-domain coordinated-agent benchmarks;
- symbolic/equation discovery;
- adversarial/refutational scientific reasoning;
- provenance-preserving scientific artifacts.

Therefore, **“AI-driven cross-domain theory research” is not itself a defensible novelty claim.**

A narrower contribution may survive: a formal, typed, **constraint-preserving theory-transfer protocol** in which a cross-domain mapping creates mandatory proof obligations and is evaluated by net validated discovery yield against strong simpler baselines. The audit did not identify a single reviewed system that clearly combines *all* of those obligations as its central scientific object, but several 2025–2026 systems approach parts of it closely. That residual gap must be treated as provisional pending deeper literature review and direct author-level comparison.

## Collision scale

- **5 — Direct collision:** implements the same central operation or claim.
- **4 — Very strong collision:** implements most of the relevant operation, missing only one or two proposed distinctions.
- **3 — Strong adjacent:** covers a major component but has a different central object.
- **2 — Supporting adjacent:** provides an enabling method or evaluation concept.
- **1 — Context only:** relevant background, not a substantive collision.

## Direct collision findings

### 1. Literature-Based Discovery already owns the foundational “hidden connection” idea

Don Swanson’s work established that two literatures can be logically connected without citing one another and that their combination can yield a new inference. Later LBD explicitly became a sub-discipline of text mining/hypothesis generation, including generalized cross-domain methods. ADTRCDF therefore cannot characterize “AI searches disconnected literatures for missing connections” as a new field-defining principle.

### 2. SciAgents and related graph work already combine AI, ontologies, cross-domain connection finding and hypothesis generation

SciAgents uses ontological knowledge graphs, LLMs, tools and multiple agents to uncover relationships among previously unrelated concepts and generate/refine scientific hypotheses. Related graph work explicitly uses transitive and isomorphic relationships to reveal interdisciplinary structures and propose designs. This collides strongly with the original “normalize -> connect -> theorize” concept.

### 3. Discovery Engine already proposes structured scientific artifacts and operations over a unified knowledge representation

The Discovery Engine proposes structured, source-linked knowledge artifacts, a universal conceptual schema, a conceptual tensor/knowledge graph, and agent operations intended to identify gaps and non-obvious connections and generate hypotheses/designs. This is the closest collision with a proposed ADTRCDF ontology + map + connection engine.

### 4. THEORIZER already occupies literature-grounded scientific theory synthesis

THEORIZER explicitly formulates “literature-based theory synthesis,” extracting structured evidence from large corpora and generating qualitative and quantitative laws. It evaluates specificity, empirical support, predictive accuracy, novelty and plausibility; literature-grounded theories predict findings in subsequently written papers better than parametric-memory generation in the reported experiments. ADTRCDF cannot claim “AI reads literature and builds theories” as novel.

### 5. Stanford’s 2026 analogical-reasoning work directly occupies cross-domain relational transfer

The Stanford work introduces an analogical-reasoning method that identifies shared relational structure between distant domains and transfers solution ideas. The authors report more diverse/novel generated solutions and implement transferred approaches across four biomedical problems. This is the strongest collision with the original proposed `S_A ≅ S_B -> transfer mechanism -> hypothesis` core.

### 6. Co-Scientist already provides adversarial multi-agent hypothesis evolution with real validation

Google’s Co-Scientist uses specialized agents to generate, reflect on, rank, evolve and meta-review hypotheses, with reported experimental validation in biomedical cases. ADTRCDF therefore cannot treat “separate explorer/skeptic agents” or iterative hypothesis tournaments as field-defining novelty.

### 7. Cross-domain benchmarking with provenance and explicit nulls is now prior art

A 2026 cross-domain coordinated-agent benchmark explicitly asks when integration across disciplines changes conclusions, distinguishes performance gains from provenance/interpretability gains, uses ablations and nulls, and stores content-addressed artifacts with SHA-256 provenance graphs. This collides with the proposed “cross-domain integration itself as the scientific object + immutable provenance + baseline comparison” concept.

### 8. Agentic Science already names the higher-level future vision

A 2025–2026 Agentic Science survey defines a prospective “Generative Architect” level in which agents create new conceptual/mathematical frameworks and uncover latent connections between disparate scientific fields. Even if this is partly prospective, it means the broad vision of AI creating cross-domain scientific frameworks is not an unoccupied conceptual territory.

## Residual gap that may still be defensible

The audit does **not** presently find one established system whose *central scientific unit* is all of the following simultaneously:

1. A typed representation of source-domain theories/mechanisms that carries variables, equations/relations, assumptions, boundary conditions, empirical bounds, and provenance.
2. An explicit cross-domain mapping that distinguishes superficial analogy from structural correspondence.
3. **Constraint preservation**: assumptions, causal requirements, units, conservation laws, and empirical bounds are transferred or explicitly declared non-transferable.
4. Automatic generation of **proof obligations** from every mapping.
5. Mandatory adversarial gates before promotion: prior-art search, contradiction search, causal/formal check, dimensional analysis where applicable, quantitative effect-size prediction, and a falsification route.
6. A lifecycle in which *successful early rejection* of bad hypotheses is rewarded as a positive scientific outcome.
7. Controlled comparison against simpler baselines to measure **net validated discovery yield per unit of expert/compute/experimental cost**, not just idea novelty.
8. A multi-domain benchmark specifically designed to test transfer of theory/mechanism under constraint rather than generic interdisciplinarity or final-answer quality.

This is the only presently defensible wedge.

### Provisional name for the methodological object

**Constraint-Preserving Cross-Domain Theory Transfer (CP-CDTT)**

The research program could therefore be reframed as:

> **Can constraint-preserving cross-domain theory transfer be formalized and shown to improve scientific discovery efficiency over existing AI-for-science and interdisciplinary-reasoning methods?**

If yes, ADTRCDF might eventually justify a named subfield. If not, CP-CDTT should be published as a method within Agentic Science / AI for Science / Literature-Based Discovery.

## What is definitely NOT novel after F1

The following candidate claims are retired:

| Retired claim | Reason |
|---|---|
| “AI can search disconnected scientific fields for hidden connections.” | Core LBD concept; modern cross-domain LBD and agents already do this. |
| “Multiple AI experts from different domains can collaborate.” | Demonstrated in cross-domain multi-agent work and scientific agent systems. |
| “Knowledge graphs can expose interdisciplinary links.” | SciAgents/Buehler and Discovery Engine directly cover this. |
| “AI can synthesize scientific theories from literature.” | THEORIZER directly formulates and evaluates this task. |
| “AI can use analogies across fields to discover new approaches.” | Stanford 2026 analogical-reasoning work directly demonstrates this. |
| “AI can debate/refine hypotheses with specialized agents.” | Co-Scientist and other multi-agent systems do this. |
| “AI can operate a hypothesis-experiment-analysis loop.” | Robin and AI Scientist systems establish this direction. |
| “Cross-domain AI research needs dedicated benchmarks.” | IDRBench, IsoSci and 2026 coordinated cross-domain benchmarks already exist. |
| “Provenance/hashing makes cross-domain agent science auditable.” | 2026 coordinated-agent work explicitly uses content-addressed SHA-256 artifact provenance. |
| “AI may eventually invent new scientific frameworks by combining disciplines.” | Explicitly articulated in Agentic Science’s prospective Generative Architect level. |

## Claims still PROVISIONAL, not cleared

1. CP-CDTT is novel as a complete protocol.
2. A theory-transfer “proof obligation” formalism is absent from prior work.
3. Constraint preservation will improve valid discovery yield.
4. Early falsification can be optimized as an explicit objective without suppressing novelty.
5. The method generalizes across physics, materials/engineering, and another non-biological domain.
6. A distinct named field is warranted rather than a contribution inside Agentic Science / AI for Science.

These require direct paper-level comparison and benchmark evidence.

## Recommended field boundary after F1

### Parent umbrellas

- AI for Science
- Agentic Science / autonomous scientific discovery
- Computational scientific discovery
- Literature-Based Discovery
- Interdisciplinary / transdisciplinary research
- Scientific knowledge representation and knowledge graphs
- Automated hypothesis generation / theory synthesis
- Computational analogy / analogical reasoning

### Candidate subproblem

**Constraint-Preserving Cross-Domain Theory Transfer (CP-CDTT)**

### Candidate long-term field label

**ADTRCDF remains a lab/project codename only.** It should not appear in public work as an established or newly invented scientific field until benchmark evidence shows that CP-CDTT constitutes a coherent, generalizable research object not already subsumed by the parent umbrellas.

## F1 terminal decision

**STATUS: FRAMEWORK_PASS_WITH_NEW_FIELD_CLAIM_BLOCKED**

Reason:

- The scientific problem is real.
- The research territory is active and rapidly advancing.
- The original broad novelty claim collides heavily with established and 2025–2026 work.
- A narrower methodological wedge remains plausible but unproven.

### Next independently judgeable unit

**F2 + F3: formalize CP-CDTT and the ontology.**

Required outputs:

1. A mathematical/typed definition of a domain theory structure.
2. A definition of legal vs illegal cross-domain transfer.
3. A proof-obligation schema generated by each transfer.
4. A small hand-built corpus containing at least 10 successful historical cross-domain transfers and 10 seductive but invalid analogies.
5. Independent evaluator test: can reviewers classify valid/invalid mappings using the formalism with high agreement?

**No agent architecture should be built before this passes.**

## Audit limitation

This is a high-coverage web and primary-source collision audit, not a formal systematic review with database-level exhaustive query logging across Scopus, Web of Science, Dimensions, Semantic Scholar, Crossref and discipline-specific indexes. The word **exhaustive** is therefore used operationally for the major directly colliding research families found in the current search, not as a bibliometric completeness claim. A publication-grade novelty claim would require a reproducible systematic-search protocol and direct full-text comparison of the closest systems.
