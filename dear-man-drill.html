STEP 1 OF 9
INTELLIGENCE BRIEF
What's the conversation?
Who is this with, and what's the core of what needs to be said? Brief. You don't need the whole story — just enough to orient the drill. This doesn't go in your script.
Examples:
"My partner about feeling shut out since I got back."
"My boss about the workload being unsustainable."
"My sponsor about the situation I haven't told them yet."
"My ex about the kids' schedule."

STEP 2 OF 9
THE SCRIPT — LETTER D
D
DESCRIBE
State the situation using only observable facts. What happened, when, and what was said or done. No interpretations. No blame. No story about why they did it.
WHY THIS MATTERS
When you lead with interpretation ("You always ignore me"), the other person defends, not listens. When you lead with fact ("Last three times I called, it went to voicemail"), they can't argue with what happened.
Not this: "You've been completely checked out since you got home."
This: "The last four evenings you came home, ate, and went straight to the basement. We haven't talked for more than ten minutes."

STEP 3 OF 9
THE SCRIPT — LETTER E
E
EXPRESS
How you feel about it. Your feelings, your experience. One or two sentences. "I feel..." — not "You make me feel..."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Most men were trained to skip this step entirely. That's why the conversation goes sideways — the other person doesn't know what's at stake for you. One honest sentence does more work than twenty minutes of arguments.
Not this: "You obviously don't care about this family."
This: "I feel like I'm losing you, and I don't know what to do with that."

Or keep it simple: "When that happens, I feel alone. And that's hard for me to say."

STEP 4 OF 9
THE SCRIPT — LETTER A
A
ASSERT
Say clearly what you want or need. Not a hint wrapped in a complaint. Not a question designed to make them guess. A direct ask.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Most failed conversations never include a clear ask. You assume they should know. They assume you would just say it. The ask bridges that gap. It's not weakness — it's precision.
Not this: "I just wish things were different." (hint)
Not this: "Don't you think we should talk more?" (guilt trap)
This: "I need us to eat dinner together three nights a week without devices. That's what I'm asking."

STEP 5 OF 9
THE SCRIPT — LETTER R
R
REINFORCE
Tell them what's in it for them if they respond well. How does this help the relationship, the situation, them specifically? Show you're not just extracting something.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Without this, the other person calculates only what they're giving up. Reinforcement shows what they gain — and signals you're thinking about the relationship, not just your need.
Examples:
"If we can do this, I'll stop disappearing in my head every evening. You'll actually have me back."
"This gives us something to hold on to when things get hard again."
"It means I won't need to keep carrying this alone. That'll make things better for both of us."

STEP 6 OF 9
THE SCRIPT — LETTER N
N
NEGOTIATE
What are you willing to flex on? What alternative would you accept? Showing willingness to work with them isn't giving in — it's what separates a negotiation from an ultimatum.
WHY THIS MATTERS
If your ask is "all or nothing," most people shut down. Offering a flex shows good faith. It also helps you clarify internally: what's truly non-negotiable versus what's just preference.
Examples:
"If three nights is too much right now, I could start with two. What feels doable to you?"
"I don't need the answer today — I just need to know you heard me and we're going to talk about it."
"If you're not ready to do it this way, tell me what you could do. I'm open to that."

STEP 7 OF 9
DURING THE CONVERSATION
M + A — The two things you can't script
These aren't lines to rehearse. They're postures to hold when the conversation doesn't go according to plan — which it won't.
M
MINDFUL — STAY ON OBJECTIVE
They will deflect. They will bring up the past. They will make it about something else. Your job is to notice that, and return.

You don't have to win every sub-argument. You have one goal in this conversation.
"I hear you. That's a fair point. And I still need to talk about what I came here to talk about."

Say it calmly. Say it as many times as it takes. Don't bite the bait.
A
APPEAR CONFIDENT
You don't need to feel ready. You need to show up anyway.

Stand or sit straight. Don't fold your arms. Keep your voice even — not louder, not smaller. Eye contact.
Confidence in this context isn't certainty that it'll go well. It's showing up like you believe you deserve to have this conversation — because you do.
