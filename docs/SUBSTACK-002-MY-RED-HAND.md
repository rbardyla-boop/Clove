# My Red Hand

_Substack draft — not yet published_

I have a bad habit when I tell stories.

I remember the part where somebody fucked me over in 4K.

The part where **I** was the asshole sometimes arrives in standard definition with a damaged antenna.

Convenient little feature of the human brain.

Everybody gets one free courtroom where they're somehow both the defendant and the fucking judge.

So before I spend the next few months poking doctors, schools, governments, universities, algorithms, masculinity theories and whatever other sacred cow wanders into traffic, I should probably put my own shit on the table.

Because I have fucked things up.

A lot.

Not movie-villain shit.

Just regular human shit.

Arrogance.

Anger.

Being too sure.

Talking when listening would have worked better.

Turning a problem into a fight because fighting was the tool I had sharpened.

Thinking competence in one role meant competence in the next one.

Hurting people while being completely convinced I was defending something important.

That's my red hand.

And I'm done pretending ownership means either:

> **I did nothing wrong.**

or:

> **Everything was my fault.**

Those are both bullshit.

## I was a good attack dog

The Army gave me one lesson I took a long time to understand.

I had become useful as the guy who would push.

Solve the problem.

Call out bullshit.

Take the hit.

Go harder.

If something needed doing, point me at it.

That can make you valuable.

It can also make you a fucking nightmare.

After I was promoted into leadership, somebody I respected gave me feedback that landed like a hammer.

The message, as I remember it, was basically:

> You were a great corporal and attack dog. That's not what this job needs. You have to build people now, not crush them.

And then came the part I really hated.

He wasn't going to keep carrying me through it.

At the time, that felt like abandonment.

Looking back, another possibility is sitting right there:

**Maybe I had become very good at a skill that had stopped being the skill required.**

That's uncomfortable.

Good.

Keep it.

Because this movement cannot become me standing on a soapbox explaining that institutions failed men while quietly deleting every time somebody tried to tell me I was being a difficult bastard.

Sometimes I was.

## Promotion did something stupid to my head

I had things going well.

Qualifications.

Performance.

Promotion.

Some outside validation.

And I got a big fucking head.

That's not a clinical diagnosis.

That's just English.

I started carrying myself like competence in one area had somehow issued me a universal licence.

It had not.

A promotion does not magically install leadership software.

Apparently nobody told my ego.

So when I talk now about the credential cult — degrees, titles, ranks, professional authority — I have to admit something annoying.

I understand the seduction.

I have felt it.

A title changes how other people treat you.

Then, if you're not careful, it changes how **you** treat your own opinion.

Suddenly your idea isn't just an idea.

It's an idea wearing rank.

Dangerous little costume.

## I can lecture the paint off a wall

My kids tell me I lecture too much.

This is outrageous slander.

I merely turn a fourteen-second question into a forty-five-minute documentary with historical context, tactical implications and an appendix.

Apparently children hate education.

But here's the problem:

Sometimes the lecture really is me trying to help.

I can see three ways a stupid choice might turn into a disaster because I've made enough stupid choices to recognize the road signs.

I want to save them the trip.

Good intention.

But sometimes a person does not need a lecture.

They need:

> Yeah. That sucks.

Or:

> What do you think you're going to do?

Or perhaps the advanced parenting technique known as **shutting the fuck up for thirty seconds.**

Still working on that one.

This matters because I keep asking institutions to listen to men before translating them into a theory.

Well, guess what?

That rule comes home with me.

If I'm explaining my kid's experience before I've actually heard it, I'm doing a small domestic version of the exact thing I'm bitching about.

Red hand.

Mine.

## I have benefited from unfairness too

Here's another one I don't particularly enjoy.

I was bullied and called ugly when I was younger.

For whatever reason, that version of me seems to have stayed in the control room long after the exterior renovations were completed.

I don't naturally think of myself as the attractive guy.

But adult life has repeatedly given me evidence that some women see me differently than I see myself.

I've watched rules bend for me.

I've watched charm work.

I've watched another man get a cold response and then received a warmer one myself.

For years, part of me filed observations like that under:

> Women can be fucking unfair.

Maybe.

But there's another explanation I have to carry too.

> **Maybe I was benefiting from the same unfairness.**

That changes the story.

I can still object to a double standard.

But I cannot honestly describe myself only as its victim if sometimes I was standing on the comfortable side of it.

That's the point of this whole exercise.

If the evidence makes me look better than I deserve, count it.

If it makes me look worse than I want, count that too.

Otherwise we're not doing research.

We're doing fucking public relations.

## I have mistaken persistence for righteousness

Persistence is probably one of my strongest traits.

It is also capable of becoming a character defect with excellent attendance.

When I think something is wrong, I don't like dropping it.

That can be useful.

It can also mean I keep pushing after everybody else has mentally left the building.

The fact that I won't quit does not prove I'm right.

It proves I won't quit.

Huge fucking difference.

I need that sentence because this project is going to involve fighting comfortable explanations.

And fighting comfortable explanations attracts a dangerous kind of person:

The guy who thinks opposition itself is proof he found the truth.

No.

Sometimes everybody disagrees with you because you're early.

Sometimes everybody disagrees with you because you're Galileo.

Sometimes everybody disagrees with you because you walked into Wendy's and started explaining monetary policy to the Frosty machine.

Opposition is not evidence of genius.

Neither is consensus proof of truth.

Bring the receipt.

## The loaded gun

I've had a song title stuck in my head before:

**That Gun Is Loaded.**

I started thinking about it as a relationship rule.

Not because every interaction is fifty-fifty.

It isn't.

Somebody can lie to you.

Abuse you.

Cheat you.

Exploit you.

Commit an act you did not cause and do not share responsibility for.

So I hate the lazy version of:

> It takes two.

No, sometimes one person does some spectacularly stupid shit all by themselves.

What I mean is different.

In an ordinary relationship, I own my trigger finger.

You own yours.

My tone.

My words.

My promises.

My temper.

My silence.

My apology.

My repair.

Those are mine.

Your side is yours.

That's where I finally landed:

> **Your side of the relationship is 100% yours. Their side is 100% theirs.**

Not fifty-fifty blame.

Full ownership of your own shit.

And that means something else people don't say enough.

You can work on yourself every fucking day and still reach a point where leaving is the responsible choice.

Because self-improvement does not obligate you to spend the rest of your life repairing a bridge while the person on the other side keeps setting the lumber on fire.

Which gives me one of my new favourite rules:

> **An apology is ownership. It is not a lifetime subscription to being the only asshole required to change.**

If I'm wrong, I owe repair.

If you're wrong, you owe repair.

If both of us are wrong, congratulations, we have a group project.

But if only one person keeps doing the work?

That's not a relationship anymore.

That's an infrastructure project.

## Trying to be better does not mean believing you're always bad

This one is harder for me.

I was taught, in different ways, to try to be better tomorrow than I was today.

I believe that.

I like it.

There's always something to fix.

Something to learn.

Something you could have handled better.

But there's a trap hidden inside self-improvement.

If your rule becomes:

> I must always identify what I did wrong,

then eventually you can become very fucking skilled at convicting yourself.

Something goes badly?

Find my failure.

Relationship breaks?

Find my failure.

Career stalls?

Find my failure.

Somebody dislikes me?

Find my failure.

That looks like accountability.

Sometimes it isn't.

Sometimes it's another self-sealing theory.

**Ryan is the problem.**

Evidence I screwed up?

Proof.

Evidence somebody else screwed up?

Well, I probably caused that somehow too.

Evidence I did something well?

Doesn't count. They were being nice.

Look at that.

I built the exact kind of theory I keep warning everybody about.

Can't lose.

Explains everything.

Therefore explains fuck-all.

So here's the correction:

> **I do not owe myself a flattering story. I owe myself an accurate one.**

That's harder.

And better.

## This is the deal I want to make with you

I'm going to tell you stories where I think doctors got things wrong.

Schools.

Institutions.

Women.

Men.

The Army.

Governments.

Experts.

Algorithms.

Maybe all of them at some point.

And because I can swear loudly and tell a good story, there is always a danger that the story feels more true than the evidence says it is.

So here's the deal.

I will show you my red hand too.

When I was arrogant, I'll say arrogant.

When I was cruel, I'll say cruel.

When I benefited from the same unfairness I'm criticizing, I'll say that.

When somebody I disliked was right about me, that goes in too.

When I change my claim, I will leave the old one visible.

When the evidence says I was full of shit, Clove gets to put that on the fucking website.

Not because humiliation is virtuous.

Because a movement that cannot embarrass its founder eventually becomes a religion.

I don't want one.

## My red hand is not your red hand

And here's the other half.

Owning my mistakes does not mean I accept every accusation handed to me.

It does not mean my sex makes me responsible for every man who ever lived.

It does not mean my skin makes me responsible for somebody else's ancestors.

It does not mean a professional title makes the other person automatically correct.

It does not mean disagreement is pathology.

It does not mean every broken relationship is fifty percent my fault.

It means:

**Show me my part.**

If it's mine, I'll carry it.

If it's yours, you carry yours.

If history owns it, put it in the history book.

If we don't know, say we don't fucking know.

That's the centre I'm trying to find.

No inherited guilt.

No inherited innocence.

No automatic hero.

No automatic villain.

Just ownership.

## So yes, my hand is red

I've fucked up.

I'll fuck up again.

Hopefully with improving technique and fewer witnesses.

But I am not building Clove because I believe I finally became one of the good people.

I don't even know what the fuck that means anymore.

I'm building it because I want a place where the question is not:

> **Who's the good guy?**

It's:

> **What happened?**

> **What's mine?**

> **What's yours?**

> **What does the evidence say?**

> **What do we fix next?**

That's enough.

Own the red hand.

Don't carry everybody else's.

And tomorrow, try not to make the same fucking mess again.
