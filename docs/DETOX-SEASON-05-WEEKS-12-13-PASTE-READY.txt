TITLE: SCHOOL IS AN ENGINEERED ENVIRONMENT
SUBTITLE: If boys keep struggling inside it, inspect the environment as well as the boy.
SCHEDULE: November 10, 2026

I had what I thought was a pretty clever idea.

Maybe boys and girls would do better if we separated them in school for a while.

Different development.

Different distractions.

Different social pressure.

Maybe boys could move more, compete more, learn differently and stop trying to impress girls every fourteen seconds.

Sounded plausible.

Then I checked the evidence.

Well, shit.

The broad version of my idea does not hold up very well.

A major 2014 meta-analysis looked at 184 studies involving about 1.6 million students from 21 countries.

The weaker, uncontrolled studies sometimes made single-sex schooling look better.

The better-controlled studies mostly found trivial differences between single-sex and coeducational schooling.

A later twenty-year scoping review reached a similar conclusion: claims that single-sex schooling by itself improves academic performance, post-secondary plans or career readiness should be treated very cautiously.

I love this result.

Not because I was right.

Because I wasn't allowed to keep being right merely because the idea felt good.

That is what I want Clove to do.

Kill my favourite theory before somebody prints it on a fucking T-shirt.

But the dead theory leaves behind a better question.

School is one of the largest engineered environments a child will ever spend time inside.

Hours every day.

Years of life.

Same-age groups.

Desks.

Schedules.

Evaluation.

Noise rules.

Movement rules.

Academic pathways.

Social hierarchy.

Adult authority.

So if a group of children repeatedly struggles inside that environment, why would we inspect only the child?

Imagine putting a thousand workers into a factory and discovering one category of worker is getting injured more often.

You would inspect the workers.

Sure.

Training.

Behaviour.

Fitness.

But you would also inspect the fucking factory.

Tools.

Ergonomics.

Shift length.

Work design.

Protective equipment.

Why would education be exempt from the same systems thinking?

This does not mean:

School is designed against boys.

I do not have evidence for that sweeping claim.

It means:

School design can interact with human differences, and those interactions are measurable.

Maybe the variables that matter are not sex segregation at all.

Movement.

Sleep.

Classroom structure.

Teacher expectations.

Reading development.

Discipline practices.

Hands-on learning.

Vocational pathways.

Mentorship.

Competition.

Collaboration.

School belonging.

Time outdoors.

The age when particular choices are forced.

Maybe some of those affect boys and girls differently.

Maybe socioeconomic class explains more than sex in some settings.

Maybe a reform that helps struggling boys helps struggling girls too.

That would be an excellent outcome.

The point is not to build boy school and girl school because biology exists.

The point is to stop treating the current classroom as though God handed Moses a tablet saying:

FORTY-SEVEN MINUTES. SIT DOWN. COMPLETE WORKSHEET.

Educational environments are human designs.

Human designs can be evaluated.

And when the evidence says my proposed redesign does not work, cross it off the list and keep moving.

That is not defeat.

That is fucking maintenance.

I still think boys deserve serious attention.

I still think we should ask whether environments fit different kinds of children differently.

I just no longer get to pretend that separating the sexes is the obvious answer.

Good.

One less slogan.

One better question.

BORING RECEIPT

Pahlke, Hyde and Allison's 2014 Psychological Bulletin meta-analysis included 184 studies and roughly 1.6 million K–12 students across 21 countries. Uncontrolled studies sometimes showed modest single-sex advantages, but controlled studies generally showed trivial differences and the highest-quality evidence did not support broad academic benefits from single-sex schooling.

A 2021 twenty-year scoping review included 70 papers from 12 countries and concluded that claims that single-sex education alone improves academic performance, post-secondary plans or career readiness should be questioned.

These findings do not prove every single-sex program is ineffective or that targeted environments cannot help particular students. They do reject treating sex separation itself as a broadly established solution.

TITLE: EIGHTEEN IS NOT A NEUROLOGICAL SWITCH
SUBTITLE: We teach a teenager to drive a car, then hand him the internet with instructions not to lose the charger.
SCHEDULE: November 13, 2026

At midnight on your eighteenth birthday, something incredible happens.

The government detects a small neurological click.

Your frontal lobe lights green.

Responsibility installs.

Terms and conditions accepted.

Adult mode unlocked.

Obviously that is not how humans work.

Legal adulthood needs a line.

Biology does not owe the legislature one.

This is where the internet usually responds with another oversimplification:

The brain isn't fully developed until 25.

Also too neat.

Brain development continues through adolescence and into young adulthood.

Different processes and regions change on different timelines.

But there is no scientifically established birthday when the entire brain becomes “finished.”

A 2025 neuroscience perspective addressing this exact claim concluded there is no empirically defined neurodevelopmental endpoint at age 25.

A 2023 Nature Communications study on executive-function development likewise warned against simplistic fixed-age models and found different developmental trajectories rather than one magic maturity switch.

So we have two facts that should be able to coexist.

Young adults are adults under the law.

Young adulthood is still a period of development.

That should not be controversial.

And I think digital life exposes how weird our preparation system is.

We make a teenager take lessons before operating a car.

Rules of the road.

Signs.

Parking.

Testing.

Supervised practice.

Then we give him a device connected to:

Sex.

Money.

Pornography.

Strangers.

Reputation.

Politics.

Gambling mechanics.

Advertising.

Infinite entertainment.

Public humiliation.

AI.

And every lunatic on Earth.

Training provided:

Don't lose the charger.

Maybe the answer is not to keep people legally childish until 25.

I do not want a neuroscience caste system where a twenty-four-year-old can work, pay taxes and raise children but somebody on TikTok declares his opinions invalid because the prefrontal cortex paperwork has not arrived.

That would be stupid.

The better analogy is licensing.

We do not delay adulthood.

We teach stewardship earlier.

Before the kid gets unlimited access to the information machine, teach him what the controls do.

What an algorithm is.

Why search ranking is not truth ranking.

How advertising works.

How pornography can become a distorted teacher.

How screenshots work.

Why private can become public.

How scams exploit urgency.

How to verify a source.

How to use AI without outsourcing judgment.

How to leave the fucking phone alone when angry.

Then add responsibility as capacity grows.

Not because young people are defective adults.

Because practice is how people become competent adults.

We already accept this everywhere else.

Apprenticeship.

Driving.

Martial arts.

Trades.

Medicine.

Military training.

Nobody says:

You will eventually be competent, therefore here is the equipment, figure it out.

Digital life somehow got a special exemption.

We connected young people to a global information, status and commerce system before we developed anything resembling universal digital driver's ed.

That is the part I want to fix.

Not with censorship.

With competence.

A nineteen-year-old does not need me standing over his shoulder forever.

He needs enough understanding that I can stop standing there.

That is what growing up is supposed to produce.

Not permanent supervision.

Agency.

BORING RECEIPT

The popular claim that the human brain becomes “fully developed” at exactly age 25 is an oversimplification. A 2025 peer-reviewed perspective concluded there is no empirically defined neurodevelopmental endpoint at 25; brain maturation is nonlinear, region-specific and extends on different timelines. Research does support continued cognitive and brain development through adolescence and into young adulthood.

The argument for digital-stewardship education is a policy/design proposal. Neuroscience alone does not dictate a particular legal age, phone policy or internet restriction.

TITLE: MEN WHO WALKED AWAY
SUBTITLE: We keep asking why men won't seek help. What about the ones who went and never came back?
SCHEDULE: November 17, 2026

There are at least four men hiding inside the sentence:

Men don't seek help.

Man one never went.

Man two went and left.

Man three went and stayed.

Man four went, left, tried something else later and finally found something that worked.

Those are not the same man.

And if we dump them into one bucket called male help-seeking, we may solve the wrong problem.

I started this whole line of thinking with a sentence I still believe:

“Ask for help” is not a treatment plan.

It is directions to the front door.

That front door matters.

Men can absolutely create their own barriers.

Pride.

Shame.

Fear of looking weak.

Not recognizing symptoms.

Thinking the problem should be handled alone.

Male-focused help-seeking research has identified those kinds of barriers and has tested strategies such as role models, practical problem-solving, mental-health literacy, positive male traits and clearer signposting to services.

Good.

Keep doing that.

But the second question begins after the appointment starts.

Why did he come back?

Or why didn't he?

Psychotherapy has a dropout problem generally.

A large meta-analysis of 669 studies and more than 83,000 clients estimated that roughly one in five clients prematurely discontinued treatment, although rates varied substantially depending on diagnosis, setting, age and how dropout was defined.

That is not a male statistic.

Important.

It tells us leaving treatment is a real clinical phenomenon across patients.

Then another meta-analysis found a moderately strong relationship between weaker therapeutic alliance and psychotherapy dropout.

Again, not male-specific.

But now we have a mechanism worth testing in men rather than guessing about them.

Do men who leave report poor alliance?

Wrong treatment preference?

Too abstract?

Too much stigma?

Not enough practical help?

Bad clinician fit?

Cost?

Scheduling?

Symptoms improving enough that they no longer want treatment?

Symptoms getting worse?

A treatment they simply hated?

A 2020 meta-analysis found that adults receiving their preferred psychosocial treatment had lower dropout and better therapeutic alliance than people who received a nonpreferred treatment or no choice.

It did not show better clinical outcomes across the board.

That is exactly the kind of distinction I want.

Preference may help keep people in the room.

That does not automatically make the preferred treatment medically better.

So the Men Who Walked Away project should not begin by asking:

How did therapy fail you?

That question already has a villain inside it.

Ask:

What happened?

Never went.

Went once.

Stayed.

Left.

Returned somewhere else.

What helped?

What hurt?

What would have made no difference?

What did the man himself contribute to the failure?

What did the service contribute?

What did another service do differently?

And include the men who say:

Therapy saved my fucking life.

They belong in the sample too.

Otherwise we are building another grievance machine.

This is not a research study yet.

It is a research question.

If Clove ever collects formal human-subject data, the privacy, ethics, consent and methodology have to exist before the recruitment poster.

Until then, the important move is conceptual.

Stop treating the door as the outcome.

Getting a man to ask for help matters.

Getting him useful help matters too.

And if he walks away, do not decide why before asking him.

BORING RECEIPT

A 2012 meta-analysis of 669 psychotherapy studies representing 83,834 clients estimated a weighted premature-discontinuation rate of 19.7%, with substantial variation across conditions and settings. A separate meta-analysis found weaker therapeutic alliance was associated with greater adult psychotherapy dropout. A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis of 29 randomized trials found adults receiving their preferred psychosocial treatment had lower dropout and better therapeutic alliance, without clear evidence of improved clinical outcomes across the other measured domains.

These findings are not evidence that men leave therapy more often than women or that poor clinician fit explains male suicide. They establish that dropout, alliance and treatment preference are legitimate variables to measure in a male-specific investigation.

TITLE: THE NEW TEMPERANCE
SUBTITLE: Get your hands back on the controls.
SCHEDULE: November 20, 2026

Temperance is an old word.

It sounds like a woman in 1893 smashing a saloon window with a Bible and the forearm strength of a commercial fisherman.

We hear it and think:

Don't drink.

Don't smoke.

Don't have fun.

Probably cover the piano legs because somebody might get ideas.

That is not what I mean by the new temperance.

I mean stewardship.

Know what has its hands on you.

Then decide whether you want them there.

Alcohol can get its hands on you.

So can gambling.

Porn.

Food.

Anger.

Validation.

Status.

Political outrage.

Shopping.

Your phone.

An algorithm.

An institution.

A guru.

An AI that agrees with every brilliant thought you have at 2 a.m.

The answer is not abstinence from everything.

I like technology.

I like food.

I like entertainment.

I like arguing.

Apparently a lot.

The question is whether the thing remains a tool or quietly becomes the driver.

That is why the chain I keep coming back to is:

Attention.

Judgment.

Agency.

Presence.

Responsibility.

Attention comes first because you cannot judge what you never noticed.

If something can repeatedly capture your attention, it gets opportunities to frame your reality.

Judgment comes next.

What is this?

Is it true?

How common is it?

Who selected it?

What is missing?

What would prove me wrong?

Then agency.

Given what I know, what do I choose?

Not what does the feed want?

Not what does my tribe demand?

Not what would make the best angry post?

What do I choose?

Then presence.

This part may be the one we lost most quietly.

You can have perfect opinions about civilization while ignoring the person sitting beside you.

You can know what happened in Washington, Ottawa, Gaza, Moscow and Beijing and not know your kid had a terrible fucking Tuesday.

The world can be in your hand while your life happens ten feet away.

Then responsibility.

What part is mine?

Not:

What part of everything that has ever happened can I be blamed for?

Not inherited guilt.

Not automatic innocence.

My part.

My attention.

My words.

My habits.

My vote.

My apology.

My repair.

My decision to stay.

My decision to leave.

My decision to put the phone down.

That is the movement I want.

Not men against women.

Not citizens against experts.

Not humans against technology.

Not left against right.

Humans against autopilot.

That is a fight everybody is eligible for.

And Clove has one job inside it.

Keep the asshole on the soapbox honest.

If I say something funny, laugh.

If I say something true, use it.

If I say something wrong, show me.

The correction is not an embarrassment to the project.

The correction is the fucking project.

I do not want your money.

I do not want to be your guru.

I do not want you replacing one set of slogans with mine.

I want you harder to bullshit.

Including by me.

And I want boys growing into men who can use powerful tools without becoming tools themselves.

People who can ask for help without surrendering agency.

People who can hear a group statistic without deleting the individual.

People who can admit they were wrong without deciding they are worthless.

People who can improve themselves without turning themselves into products.

People who can carry a phone containing half of human civilization without letting the machine carry them.

That is temperance.

Not purity.

Not prohibition.

Control.

Or maybe better:

Stewardship.

Know the machine.

Know yourself.

Know when either one is full of shit.

Get your hands back on the controls.
