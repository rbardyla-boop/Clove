# Rub Dirt on It Is Not a Health Strategy

_Substack draft — not yet published_

My wife says something to me all the time:

> **Rub some dirt on it. You're fine.**

Usually she's joking.

But the more I think about men's health, the more that stupid little sentence starts looking like public policy.

Men are told to be tough.

Walk it off.

Deal with it.

Don't make a scene.

Don't be a pussy.

Then we turn around and tell men:

> **Ask for help.**

Good.

I agree.

But "ask for help" is not a treatment plan.

It's directions to the front door.

And I have a stupid question.

> **What happens after the man walks through it?**

That matters because Canada has finally admitted something pretty important.

Men and boys have distinct health problems.

Health Canada says Canadian males lived about 80 years in 2024 compared with about 84.3 years for females. Men experience higher rates of premature death and suicide. Men account for about 70% of opioid-related deaths nationwide. And 65% of Canadian men wait more than six days after symptoms appear before seeing a doctor.

Canada is now building its first Men and Boys' Health Strategy.

Good.

About fucking time.

But here's where I want to be annoying.

If the problem is that men don't ask for help, then study why they don't ask.

Absolutely.

Shame.

Pride.

Fear.

Money.

Work.

Culture.

Bad experiences.

Whatever is actually there.

But also study the men who **did** ask.

The ones who went once and never went back.

The ones who stayed.

The ones who found somebody who changed their life.

The ones who felt misunderstood.

The ones who tried one service, quit, then found another one years later that actually worked.

Because if we're only studying the men standing outside the door, we might be missing what happens on the other side.

## I've been on both sides of that door

I've had good care.

I want that said clearly.

I've had professionals help me with things I was not fixing on my own.

I've had a psychologist do good work with my PTSD and general anxiety.

I've also had medical and mental-health interactions where I walked away thinking:

> **Did you hear what I said, or did you hear the category you already had ready for me?**

Those are not the same thing.

One of the reasons this matters to me came from talking about the death of a seventeen-year-old boy.

Jordan DeMay was sexually extorted online.

At the time I had teenage sons.

The question eating at me was not some grand political theory.

It was a father question.

> **How does a boy become so ashamed and scared that dying feels easier than walking into the next room and saying, "Dad, I fucked up and I need help"?**

That's the thing that got me.

The shame.

The isolation.

The idea that a terrible mistake had somehow become unrecoverable.

During that therapy conversation, the discussion moved toward Jordan Peterson, Andrew Tate and harmful or toxic influences.

I remember thinking:

> **How the fuck did we get here?**

Now, memory is not a security camera.

I could remember wording wrong.

The psychologist could have been talking about something broader than I understood.

She could have been expressing her own professional view.

She could have been accurately describing guidance from the provincial regulator.

So I did something boring.

I asked the regulator.

I wrote the College of Psychologists and Behaviour Analysts of Ontario and asked whether it had actually issued or promoted guidance around those figures, masculinity, radicalization or related topics.

I also asked what safeguards exist before one man's problem gets interpreted through a generalized theory about men.

And I asked the question that keeps showing up in almost everything I write:

> **What would prove the interpretation wrong?**

Because this is where human systems can get dangerous.

If a man disagrees and disagreement is proof...

If he gets angry and anger is proof...

If he leaves and leaving is proof...

If he goes quiet and silence is proof...

Congratulations.

Your theory can never fucking lose.

And if your theory explains every possible answer, your theory explains fuck-all.

## This is not me blaming therapists

That would be easy.

And stupid.

Some men absolutely avoid help because they were taught that needing help means weakness.

Some men lie to themselves.

Some drink instead of dealing with things.

Some turn anger into a personality.

Some won't go to a doctor until one of their organs starts sending registered mail.

I know this species.

I'm in it.

The point is not:

> **Men are perfect and the system is the problem.**

The point is:

> **What part of this mess belongs to the man, and what part belongs to the system he's being told to trust?**

Both questions matter.

That's what equality should mean to me.

Not pretending everybody is identical.

Not giving everyone the same wrench and calling it fair.

If women needed medicine to stop treating them like smaller men, that made sense.

If men have different patterns of suicide, addiction, preventive care, help-seeking, social connection and treatment engagement, then maybe some services need to understand men better too.

That is not taking anything from women.

It is finishing the job.

> **Women were never defective men. Men are not defective women.**

## The old message and the new message are colliding

Here's the weird place men are standing now.

Old message:

> **Rub some dirt on it.**

New message:

> **Open up. Ask for help.**

Good.

But if a man finally does it and the experience confirms every reason he was scared to go in the first place, what do you think happens next?

He tells another man.

That man tells another one.

A father tells a son.

A veteran tells another veteran.

A tradesman tells the guy beside him at lunch.

And pretty soon "ask for help" is competing with:

> **Yeah, I tried that. They didn't understand what the fuck I was saying.**

I do not know how common that experience is.

That's the point.

I want to know.

If it is rare, show me.

If it is common, show me.

If men mainly leave care because of stigma, show me.

If therapeutic fit matters, show me.

If male patients sometimes do better with male providers in certain settings, study it.

If sex makes no difference somewhere, great. Stop guessing and use the result.

I am tired of everybody arriving with the answer before we've finished asking the question.

## What Clove is going to do

I'm building Clove around a simple idea:

> **Bring the busted theory into the shop and put it on the bench.**

We're going to ask why men don't seek help.

Then we're going to ask why some men seek help and leave.

We're going to look for the programs that actually work.

We're going to look for things I don't want to find.

We're going to compare male-responsive approaches with one-size-fits-all approaches.

We're going to look at mentoring, competence, service, trades, martial arts, fatherhood, peer support, digital life and the messages boys are actually receiving.

And when one of my favourite theories gets its ass kicked by the evidence, we're putting that on the website too.

Because this cannot become another movement where the founder is always right.

Nobody gets tenure in being right.

Especially me.

## And no, I'm not selling you the answer

There is no paid tier behind this.

No premium truth.

No "subscribe for the secret method."

No $19.99 monthly plan to discover that your father should have hugged you more.

I'm here because I think boys and men are in trouble and because I am stubborn enough to keep talking about it.

My kids say I lecture too much.

Fine.

I found a bigger room.

Clove stays free.

The receipts stay visible.

If you think I'm wrong, good.

Bring evidence.

If you're a man who asked for help and it worked, I want that story.

If you asked and walked away, I want that story too.

If you're a doctor, psychologist, teacher, researcher, father, mother or somebody who thinks I'm completely full of shit, come explain why.

Just don't bring me another slogan.

We have enough slogans.

Men need help.

Some men need to get better at asking for it.

And maybe the people waiting on the other side of the door need to get better too.

Both things can be true.

> **Rub dirt on it is not a health strategy.**

Neither is yelling "ask for help" louder.

Let's find out what actually fucking works.

## Boring receipt

Health Canada says men in Canada face distinct health challenges. Its current Men and Boys' Health Strategy page reports male life expectancy at 80.0 years in 2024 compared with 84.3 years for females; higher male rates of premature death and suicide; about 70% of opioid-related deaths occurring among men; and lower use of primary and preventive care, including a reported 65% of Canadian men waiting more than six days with symptoms before seeing a doctor.

Canada's first federal Men and Boys' Health Strategy is scheduled for fall 2026. Health Canada says the strategy will address supportive environments, stigma, and encouraging men to seek help.

Those disparities do **not** prove that health-service design caused male suicide, overdose, shorter life expectancy, or treatment avoidance. They justify asking which causes and interventions are actually supported by evidence.
