# Where Did the Boys Go?

Status: RESEARCH BRIEF / NO CAUSAL VERDICT
Date: 2026-08-22

## Public question

> Something happened to boys and young men. Before we pick a villain, when did the gaps actually appear, what changed around them, and which explanations survive the timeline?

## Purpose

This project is the first large research-led investigation under Clove Temperance. It is not designed to prove that feminism, universities, women, government, technology, labour-market change, family structure, education policy, or any other preferred explanation caused male decline.

The job is to reconstruct the timeline first, then test explanations against it.

## Core rule

> You are seeing something real. Slow the fuck down before deciding why it happened.

## Primary outcomes to track

Where comparable data permit, reconstruct long-run male/female trends in:

1. school achievement and completion;
2. post-secondary enrolment;
3. post-secondary completion;
4. graduate/professional education;
5. employment and labour-force participation;
6. earnings and occupational distribution;
7. NEET/disconnection measures;
8. marriage, partnership and fatherhood;
9. suicide and preventable mortality;
10. overdose/substance mortality;
11. incarceration/justice-system contact;
12. homelessness/housing instability;
13. primary/preventive healthcare use;
14. mental-health treatment entry, continuation and dropout where available;
15. social connection/loneliness where comparable measures exist.

## Required cuts

Primary: sex.

Secondary where sample quality and comparability support them:
- age;
- race/ethnicity;
- class/income;
- region;
- urban/rural;
- education level;
- immigration generation;
- occupation/industry.

Do not force subgroup analysis when the data cannot support it.

## Timeline method

For every outcome:

1. Find the longest trustworthy series available.
2. Identify when the sex gap first appears, reverses, widens, narrows, or changes slope.
3. Mark major candidate policy, economic, educational, technological, demographic and cultural changes.
4. Reject explanations that occur after the trend they supposedly caused unless they plausibly explain acceleration rather than origin.
5. Distinguish level effects from trend effects.
6. Separate absolute male deterioration from relative female improvement.
7. Record uncertainty and breaks in measurement.

## Candidate explanation families

These are hypotheses, not conclusions.

## Adversarial hypotheses

The project must actively test alternatives that could defeat preferred explanations, including:

- many apparent male declines are actually female gains with stable male outcomes;
- economic restructuring explains more than cultural change;
- class explains more than sex in some domains;
- some male problems predate modern gender-policy changes;
- some male-targeted interventions do not outperform generic services;
- single-sex schooling does not generally improve outcomes;
- some traditional masculine norms genuinely worsen help-seeking or health;
- measured disadvantages may be concentrated in subgroups rather than men generally;
- public attention may lag outcomes without causing them.

## Stop conditions

Do not publish a causal claim when:

- trend timing contradicts it;
- the series changes definitions materially without adjustment;
- correlation is being represented as causation;
- male relative decline is being confused with male absolute decline;
- subgroup findings are generalized to all men;
- U.S. findings are silently treated as Canadian findings or vice versa;
- a political quote is being used as population evidence;
- a viral post is being used as proof rather than as a signal that a question resonates.

## Public posture

> I am not asking you to believe young men were purged, abandoned, feminized, radicalized, spoiled, weakened, or forgotten. I am asking something more basic: show me when the numbers moved. Then we can argue about why.
