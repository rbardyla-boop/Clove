# Clove Temperance Movement — Launch Record

Status: PROPOSED FOR REVIEW / NOT YET MERGED TO MAIN
Date: 2026-08-22

## Public purpose

Clove Temperance is a public-interest movement about attention, judgment, agency, competence, belonging, and men’s health.

The immediate flagship campaign is men and boys’ health in Canada during development of the federal Men and Boys’ Health Strategy.

Core proposition:

> Equal does not mean identical. Boys and men should not be treated as defective women, inherited criminals, or problems waiting to happen. They also do not need automatic praise. They need environments that help them become capable, connected, useful, responsible adults.

## Public-face role

Ryan Bardyla is the public face and storyteller.

Voice model:

> Ryan talks. The receipts keep Ryan honest.

Ryan may use profanity, comedy, lived experience, and obvious storytelling exaggeration. Material factual claims must be bounded by source evidence.

## Movement boundaries

This movement is not:

- anti-woman;
- a call to reverse women’s rights or health gains;
- a claim that all men are victims;
- a claim that masculinity is always beneficial;
- a replacement for professional medical or mental-health care;
- a pile-on mechanism against individual clinicians or institutions;
- an excuse for inherited innocence or collective blame.

## Core doctrine

1. Equal worth does not require identical treatment.
2. No inherited guilt. No inherited innocence.
3. The person can overrule the category.
4. A useful human judgment must remain capable of being proved wrong.
5. Ask for help when needed; retain agency when receiving help.
6. Build competence, belonging, service, and responsibility rather than only messaging self-esteem.
7. Technology should extend judgment, not replace it.
8. If evidence defeats the claim, change the claim.

## Current Canadian evidence anchors

### ESTABLISHED PUBLIC STATISTICS

Health Canada reports:

- male life expectancy in 2024: 80.0 years; female: 84.3 years;
- men experience higher premature death and suicide;
- men account for about 70% of opioid-related deaths nationwide;
- 65% of Canadian men wait more than six days with symptoms before visiting a doctor;
- among young men aged 15–24, very good/excellent self-rated mental health fell from 70% in 2012 to 52% in 2022; mood disorders rose 11% to 16% and generalized anxiety disorder 4% to 10%.

Statistics Canada reports:

- male life expectancy 80.03 and female life expectancy 84.29 in 2024;
- in 2022, 2,688 male versus 905 female suicide deaths;
- age-standardized preventable mortality in 2021 was substantially higher in males than females.

The Office of the Federal Ombudsperson for Victims of Crime recommends that Canada’s 2026 Men and Boys’ Health Strategy include male-responsive victim services, peer support, group programming, culturally relevant support, mentorship, and work on barriers to help-seeking.

### LIMITS

These outcome gaps do not establish one cause. They cannot by themselves show that service design, sex stereotypes, masculinity norms, biology, economics, work, isolation, or any other single factor caused the differences.

## Research docket

### 1. Men Who Walked Away

Question: Why do some men never seek care, seek care and leave, stay, or leave one service and later succeed with another?

Status: listening-project concept only. Any formal human-subject research must undergo ethics/privacy review before recruitment or claims.

### 2. Male Message Audit

Question: What do public messages aimed at boys and men actually say?

Candidate coding axes:

- male as risk;
- male as victim;
- male as agent;
- male as problem;
- male as resource;
- positive masculinity;
- historical/collective guilt;
- individual responsibility;
- competence;
- service;
- fatherhood;
- belonging;
- help-seeking;
- shame/blame;
- strength framed positively/negatively.

### 3. Male-Responsive Care Tests

Question: Does changing language, setting, framing, peer involvement, practical problem-solving, or activity improve trust, return, engagement, or outcomes?

### 4. Clinical Framing Test

Question: Do otherwise-identical clinical cases receive different interpretations when cues such as sex, veteran status, trade work, anger, politics, or named influencers are introduced?

### 5. Competence and Belonging

Question: Which real-world activities help boys and men build competence, confidence, responsibility, connection, and help-seeking capacity?

Candidate settings:

- mentoring;
- martial arts;
- trades;
- service/volunteering;
- building projects;
- father-son work;
- sport;
- peer groups.

### 6. Digital Temperance

Question: What happens when technology removes the delay between impulse and action or substitutes visible optimization for slow competence-building?

## Stop conditions

Do not publish or repeat a claim when:

- the source cannot be located;
- the claim materially exceeds the source;
- a lived-memory detail is being presented as documentary fact;
- a group-level finding is being applied as a verdict on an individual;
- a criticism requires guessing motive;
- a named private person would bear disproportionate harm relative to the public-interest value.
