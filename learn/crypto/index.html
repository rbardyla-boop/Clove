CLOVELEARN REFERENCE · CRYPTO MECHANICS · EDUCATION ONLY

Crypto without the casino.

No price charts. No trading tips. No “next 100x coin.” Just what the machinery actually does.

You do not need to buy cryptocurrency to understand cryptocurrency. Start with the town deed office, then open the machinery underneath it.

Understand the machinery before judging the product. Blockchain can establish ledger state. Cryptography can establish authorization. Consensus can establish an accepted history. None of them can prove economic value.

01 · START HERE

The town with no town hall

A town deed office keeps a shared property book. Now remove the town hall, give every participating office a copy, and ask the offices to agree on which requests belong in the next page. That is the teaching device.

PLAIN-ENGLISH TOUR

Somebody presses Send

A request arrives: “Move this recorded thing from this key-holder to that key-holder.” The network does not ask whether the lunch was good, whether the buyer understood the risks, or whether the thing is worth anything. It checks whether the request follows the rules and whether the right signing authority approved it.

Copies of the book compare notes. A mechanism for ordering competing pages decides which history the network accepts. The deed office can say, “This request is valid under our rules.” It cannot say, “This was a wise decision.”

THE METAPHOR HELPS

  • Shared records make conflicting requests visible.
  • Signatures show control of a key.
  • Consensus selects an accepted history.
  • Many copies reduce dependence on one clerk.

THE METAPHOR FAILS

  • Real property exists inside law and courts.
  • Bitcoin does not receive ownership validity from a land-title authority.
  • A ledger record is not automatically a legal claim to an off-chain asset.
  • Technical validity is not economic value.
LEDGER STATEWhat the network records

Which outputs, balances, or contract state are currently accepted under protocol rules.

CRYPTOGRAPHYWho authorized data

Whether a valid signature or integrity check matches the relevant key or message.

CONSENSUSWhich history wins

How independent participants converge on an accepted ordering or finalized state.

DAD VERSION What problem is a blockchain trying to solve?

Several computers need to maintain a shared record without trusting one master bookkeeper. They need rules for accepting transactions, rejecting invalid ones, ordering competing requests, and recovering when participants disagree or disappear.

SHOW ME THE MACHINERY A blockchain is not one universal architecture

“Blockchain” is a family resemblance, not a single machine. Networks differ in state model, consensus, validator selection, execution, finality, privacy, client software, and governance. A node usually stores and checks data; a consensus mechanism helps participants converge on ordering or finality; applications may add another trust layer on top.

NERD HATCH Genesis, headers, Merkle roots, forks, and finality

A block can contain a header, transaction data, and commitments such as a Merkle root. A header can commit to a previous block hash, creating a linked history. A fork is competing history or a rule change; a confirmation is not identical to finality. “Immutable” is shorthand for “increasingly difficult or costly to alter under this network’s current security model,” not a law of physics.

KEYS, ADDRESSES, WALLETS

A wallet is a keyring, not a bag of coins

A private key is secret signing authority. A public key helps verify a signature. An address is a network-specific representation used to identify a destination or spending condition. Wallet software manages keys and presents a usable view of network state.

A recovery phrase can encode or derive key material, which is why it is a secret. This page never asks for one. Self-custody means you carry the recovery responsibility; a custodian carries it for you, along with its own failure modes.

NERD HATCH · Bitcoin UTXOs

Bitcoin does not fundamentally maintain one bank-style balance per address. Transactions consume unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs) and create new outputs. Wallet software adds the pieces into a balance that humans can use.

SIGNATURES

Authorization is not identity, wisdom, or value

A valid digital signature can establish that the holder of a relevant key authorized a particular message and that the signed data was not changed. It does not establish a real-world identity by itself, informed consent, legality, good judgment, solvency, or economic value.

Cryptography can authenticate stupidity perfectly.

HASHES

A fingerprint, not a locked box

A cryptographic hash maps input to a fixed-looking digest. The same input gives the same output; a small input change usually produces a very different digest. Hashes support integrity checks and block linking. They are not encryption: there is no secret key that “unlocks” a hash.

Try the local lab below. Nothing leaves this browser.

THE LEDGER DOESN’T KNOW

Not my department

A blockchain may know that fictional Address A transferred 10 tokens to fictional Address B. It does not inherently know whether Bob was scammed, whether a promoter lied, whether an off-chain asset exists, or whether the person pressing the button understood the deal.

The ledger can answer “did this follow the rules?” It cannot answer “was this a good idea?”

03 · NETWORK TYPES

Different machines, different trade-offs

Do not turn architecture into a winner’s podium. A design can be useful for one purpose and introduce serious assumptions somewhere else.

BITCOIN · PROOF OF WORK

Expensive competition to propose the next page

Miners assemble candidate blocks and repeatedly hash block-header candidates until one satisfies the current target. The successful miner proposes a block; independent nodes still validate transactions and consensus rules. Rewards combine a block subsidy and transaction fees.

NERD HATCH · accumulated work

Each block adds work that makes rewriting a long history more expensive under the current rules. Difficulty adjusts over time. The security benefit of the resource cost and the environmental/resource trade-off are separate questions.

ETHEREUM · PROOF OF STAKE

Collateral-backed validator participation

Validators deposit stake, propose or attest to blocks, and can receive rewards or penalties. Ethereum documentation describes a 32 ETH deposit for solo validator participation; pools and services can structure participation differently. Specified dishonest actions can lead to slashing.

NERD HATCH · slots, epochs, finality

Ethereum organizes consensus into slots and epochs. Attestations help the protocol evaluate chain choice and finality. “Staked” does not mean “risk-free”; software, key, operator, governance, and penalty assumptions remain.

SMART CONTRACTS

“Executed as written” is not “safe”

Smart-contract code changes state when a transaction triggers it. Gas or fees account for computation and scarce blockspace. The code may depend on an oracle, an upgradeable proxy, an admin key, a governance vote, or another contract.

Deterministic execution can reproduce a bug with impressive consistency.

NATIVE ASSETS AND TOKENS

“Token” is a family word

A native asset belongs to a network’s base rules. A fungible token is usually contract-defined. An NFT is non-fungible. A governance token may represent voting rules. A meme coin is a cultural/economic label, not a technical security guarantee. Tokenised securities and deposits introduce legal and issuer layers.

STABLECOINS

Stable in the label is not stable in every layer

Reserve-backed, crypto-collateralised, algorithmic, and tokenised-bank-deposit designs rely on different mechanisms. Separate the blockchain transfer from issuer redemption, reserve custody, legal claim, liquidity, governance, and operational risk.

A stablecoin can move on a decentralised network while depending on a highly centralised issuer.

LAYER 2s AND BRIDGES

More layers mean more assumptions

Rollups and other Layer 2 systems move some execution, ordering, or batching away from a base layer and settle back according to their design. Sequencers, data availability, upgrade keys, and withdrawal paths matter. Bridges connect systems that do not automatically share state, adding another trust and failure surface.

04 · SECURITY AND FAILURE MODES

The machine can be valid and still fail you

Security is a stack of assumptions. A protocol rule, a key, an operator, a bridge, an issuer, a website, and a human can each introduce a different failure.

KEY

Private-key theft

If the signing secret is stolen, the ledger may accept an unauthorized-to-you but cryptographically valid request.

HUMAN

Phishing and consent

A person can be tricked into authorizing something they did not understand. A valid signature does not repair deception.

CODE

Contract bug

Smart-contract code can execute deterministically while encoding a dangerous or mistaken rule.

BRIDGE

Cross-system failure

A bridge must establish that an event occurred elsewhere. Its proof, operators, keys, and upgrade path matter.

ISSUER

Redemption or reserve failure

A token’s transfer mechanics do not guarantee that an issuer can redeem it or that an off-chain reserve exists.

GOVERNANCE

Admin or rule change

Forks, upgrades, multisigs, and governance can alter what a community treats as the accepted system.

CUSTODY

Convenience moves responsibility around

Self-custody puts recovery and key safety on the holder. Exchange or regulated custody puts operational responsibility with an institution, while adding counterparty, access, and insolvency assumptions. Hardware wallets and multisignature schemes reduce some risks and add setup/recovery complexity. None require live funds on this page.

DECENTRALISATION

It is not one switch

Ask separately about node distribution, miner or validator concentration, client diversity, developer and governance power, RPC/infrastructure dependence, custody concentration, issuer concentration, and sequencer concentration.

FORKS AND UPGRADES

Software rules still need social coordination

A soft fork changes rules in a backward-compatible direction; a hard fork can create incompatible rules and potentially a chain split. People choose software, operators coordinate, and economic activity follows the history participants accept.

DEFENSIVE THREAT MAP

Know the category, not the exploit recipe

Double spending, majority hashpower attacks, validator equivocation, phishing, buggy contracts, bridge compromise, exchange insolvency, reserve failure, oracle failure, and admin-key abuse are different failure classes. This page names them without teaching anyone how to attack a live system.

05 · THE BANKING MESSAGE LAYER

ISO 20022 is the language. A token can be the thing moved.

ISO 20022 is a financial message scheme and modelling approach. It is not a blockchain, a settlement token, a global central-bank ledger, or an official list of approved coins.

MYTH / OVERCLAIM

There is no magic “ISO 20022 coin list.”

The official ISO 20022 FAQ says cryptocurrencies are not inherently ISO 20022 compliant and are not managed or registered by ISO 20022. A company can build an ISO 20022-compatible interface around a blockchain; that does not make the blockchain protocol or token “ISO-approved.”

ESTABLISHED · MESSAGE STANDARD

Better labels are not the cargo

The standard provides common financial business semantics, data dictionaries, and message definitions. SWIFT reported the end of the MT/ISO 20022 coexistence period for cross-border payment instructions on 22 November 2025. Payments Canada describes ISO 20022 as the standard for electronic payment messages across its infrastructure, including Lynx.

EMERGING / PILOT · SETTLEMENT INFRASTRUCTURE

Tokenisation is a separate layer

Project Agorá explores tokenised commercial-bank deposits and tokenised central-bank money on programmable infrastructure, including atomic multi-currency settlement. The Bank of Canada joined the project’s next phase in 2026. That is regulated institutions testing possible settlement designs—not proof that banks run on Bitcoin, XRP, stablecoins, or any public crypto.

06 · GLOSSARY AND RECEIPTS

        
Words worth keeping separate

        
When a sentence collapses several layers into one exciting noun, slow down and ask which layer it describes.

      

      

        
Block
A bounded batch of data, often with a header and transactions, proposed according to a network’s rules.

        
Consensus
The rules and processes participants use to converge on accepted ordering or state.

        
Finality
A protocol’s guarantee or strong expectation that a state will not be reverted under specified assumptions.

        
Gas
Ethereum’s accounting for computation and other resource use; the fee paid can vary with demand and design.

        
Hash
A deterministic digest used for integrity, commitments, and linking; not encryption.

        
Node
Software that participates in a network by receiving, checking, storing, relaying, or executing according to its role.

        
Oracle
A system that supplies external data to on-chain code. The chain can check the supplied data’s format, not automatically its real-world truth.

        
Tokenisation
Representing a claim or asset in token form on a digital platform. The legal claim, issuer, custody, and redemption still matter.

        
UTXO
An unspent transaction output in Bitcoin’s transaction model: a discrete output available to be consumed by a later transaction.

THE BOTTOM LINE

      
Keep your hands on the controls.

      
Blockchain can prove protocol state. Cryptography can prove authorization. Consensus can establish a shared history. ISO 20022 can standardize the instructions financial systems exchange. Tokenisation can change how some forms of money and assets are represented and settled.

      
None of those systems can prove the asset is valuable, the promoter is honest, or the human pressing the button has made a good decision.

      Understand the machinery. Then keep your hands on the controls.
