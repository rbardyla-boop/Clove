CORRECTIONS · VERSION HISTORY · NO QUIET MEMORY HOLES

If the evidence changes, the page changes.

Being correction-friendly means more than editing a sentence and pretending it was always there. Material changes should leave a trail.

Correction rule

When a material factual claim is wrong, misleading, materially incomplete, or stronger than the evidence supports, CloveLearn should correct it.

The correction should say what changed, why it changed, and what evidence forced the change.

What counts as material?

A typo usually is not a correction event. A wrong number, misquoted source, false attribution, missing denominator, unsupported causal claim, outdated legal statement, or omitted counterevidence can be.

Correction levels

Minor edit — spelling, formatting, broken link replacement, or wording that does not change the factual meaning.

Clarification — the original wording was defensible but easy to misread. The claim is narrowed or explained.

Correction — a material factual statement was wrong or overstated and is changed.

Retraction / withdrawal — the core claim cannot be defended responsibly. The page should say so rather than quietly disappearing.

What a correction note should contain

DATE

ORIGINAL CLAIM

WHAT CHANGED

WHY

NEW SOURCE / COUNTEREVIDENCE

CURRENT STATUS

No side gets immunity

A correction is not a defeat for the project. Refusing a correction because it is politically inconvenient would be.

Founder rule

Nobody gets tenure in being right, especially the founder.

A system that cannot embarrass its own author eventually becomes a loyalty test. CloveLearn is supposed to do the opposite.
