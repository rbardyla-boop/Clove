The New Temperance · CloveLearn

Rub dirt on it is not a health strategy.

Men are being told to ask for help. Good. Now we need to make damn sure the help can hear them when they arrive.

Equal does not mean identical. Fair is not a photocopier.

Why now?

Canada is building its first federal Men and Boys’ Health Strategy for fall 2026. The government itself says men and boys face distinct health challenges and that some of those problems have been overlooked, misunderstood, or ignored.

80.0 vs 84.3Life expectancy at birth in 2024: males versus females in Canada.
Almost 3×Males were almost three times as likely to die by suicide as females in 2022.
70%Health Canada reports men account for about 70% of opioid-related deaths nationwide.
65%Health Canada reports 65% of Canadian men wait more than six days with symptoms before visiting a doctor.

Those numbers do not tell us one simple cause. Biology, work, culture, risk, isolation, health behaviour, economics, service design, and other factors can all matter. That is the point: stop pretending one slogan explains the whole machine.

“Ask for help” is not a treatment plan. It is directions to the front door.

We are not swinging the pendulum at women.

We are trying to get it back near centre.

Women were right to demand that medicine, workplaces, and institutions stop treating them like defective men. Boys and men deserve the same courtesy: stop treating them like defective women.

Equal worth does not require identical biology. Equal rights do not require identical needs. A sex difference is not an insult. It is a question to investigate.

The pendulum is not a wrecking ball. Put the fucking level down and read the bubble.

The problem we want to test

For years the public message has often been: men need to open up, talk more, seek care earlier, and stop trying to handle everything alone.

There is evidence behind parts of that message. Masculine self-stigma and some traditional norms can make help-seeking harder. But that cannot become a self-sealing explanation for every man who never enters care, leaves care, or says the help did not fit.

We want to study both sides of the door:

Why did he not go?

Shame, cost, access, fear, work, identity, stigma, distrust, lack of information, or something else?

Why did he leave?

Was the service useful? Did he feel understood? Was the approach practical? Did the therapeutic relationship work?

Why did he stay?

What did successful care do differently? What made him trust it enough to return?

What actually helps?

Peer support, mentorship, activity, direct problem-solving, counselling, medical care, group programs, digital tools, or combinations?

Things worth repeating until they become annoying

“Rub dirt on it is not a national health strategy.”

Toughness can be useful. Neglect is not toughness.

“The door says HELP. Great. Does anybody answer when a man knocks?”

Access is not the same as fit, trust, or effective care.

“Men are not broken women. Women were never broken men.”

Equal dignity. Real differences. Better design.

“If your theory explains every answer, your theory explains fuck-all.”

Good human judgment must remain capable of being proved wrong.

“You cannot shame a boy into healthy masculinity.”

Give him standards, belonging, responsibility, competence, and someone worth learning from.

“Don’t just tell a boy he matters. Put him somewhere his contribution matters.”

Belonging gets stronger when it has a job to do.

What Clove will build and test

Men Who Walked Away

A careful listening project comparing men who never sought help, left early, stayed, or found better care later. Formal research claims require proper ethics and methodology.

Male Message Audit

Collect real public messages aimed at boys and men and code what they actually say: risk, shame, responsibility, competence, help, belonging, strength, historical guilt, and positive masculinity.

Male-Responsive Care Tests

Compare different ways of framing and delivering help. Measure return, trust, usefulness, and outcomes instead of asking whether people liked the poster.

Competence & Belonging

Test practical environments: mentoring, martial arts, trades, service, building projects, father-son work, peer groups, and real responsibility.

Digital Temperance

Rebuild the gap between impulse and action. Phones and AI should extend human judgment, not replace it.

Receipt Ledger

Every public claim gets a source, a confidence level, a limitation, and something that could prove it wrong.

The Temperance Pledge

  • No inherited guilt. No inherited innocence.
  • No sex gets automatic sainthood or automatic blame.
  • Equal does not mean identical.
  • The person gets to overrule the category.
  • Evidence beats slogans, including ours.
  • Ask for help when you need it. Keep your agency when you get it.
  • Build competence, belonging, service, and real-world responsibility.
  • Technology can help you think. It does not get to decide what you believe.
  • If we are wrong, change the fucking claim.

Start here

We are not asking for a sex war. We are asking for better questions, better evidence, and systems that can hear men without first deciding what kind of men they are.
