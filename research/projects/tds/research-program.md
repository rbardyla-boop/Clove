# Democracy, Information, Liberty & Influence
## Public Research Programme v0.1

### Project rule

Do not prove that democracy is fake, that voters are controlled, that elites run everything, or that algorithms determine elections.

Investigate the competing claims.

> What happens when formal political agency expands while the systems capable of selecting, interpreting, measuring, targeting and responding to human judgment become progressively more sophisticated?

The Dissenter is a narrative pressure-test, not an authority. Strong claims are converted into research questions and tested against history, political theory, behavioural science and institutional evidence.

## Common output discipline

Every substantial research window should return:

1. BLUF — what survives, what fails, confidence.
2. Claim ledger — claim / evidence for / evidence against / alternative / status.
3. Primary-source timeline — dates, documents, significance.
4. Best evidence against the preferred story.
5. Myths not to repeat.
6. Quantitative evidence — denominators, sample sizes, effect sizes, definitions.
7. Canada/U.S. separation — no silent jurisdiction transfer.
8. Narrative-grade scenes — FACT / REPORTED / INFERENCE / INTERPRETATION / UNKNOWN.
9. Falsifiers — what evidence would materially change the conclusion?
10. Source library — primary > peer-reviewed/university press > high-quality synthesis > commentary.

## Empirical Gauntlet

The experimental programme treats these as hypotheses, not discoveries:

1. Source Contamination.
2. Missing Denominator.
3. Social Proof / Cascade.
4. Moral Emergency Override.
5. Correction Order.
6. Personalized Selection / Ranking.

Each proposed mechanism needs a hypothesis, null, smallest effect of interest, manipulation, outcomes, confounds, demand-characteristic analysis, sample plan, preregistration, exclusions, equivalence test, false-positive control, mirror condition, ethics plan and replication rule.

The design target is not statistical significance. It is falsifiability.

## Merge gate

Assign every consequential claim one of these states:

- **ESTABLISHED**
- **STRONG**
- **PLAUSIBLE**
- **SPECULATIVE**
- **REJECTED**

For **STRONG** or **ESTABLISHED** claims, preserve a primary source when applicable, a high-quality secondary source, and the strongest serious counterargument.

For **PLAUSIBLE** claims, use conditional language and identify missing evidence.

For **SPECULATIVE** claims, keep them out of declarative narrative unless explicitly framed as hypotheses.

For **REJECTED** claims, preserve them in a myths-we-killed ledger rather than silently deleting them.

## Working north star

Do not prove:

> Democracy is fake.

Investigate:

> How does formal individual political agency operate inside institutions and information systems that select choices, convert votes into power, constrain dissent, measure citizens and increasingly adapt to their behaviour?

Final reader question:

> What do I actually mean when I say I made up my own mind?
