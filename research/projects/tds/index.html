ACTIVE · ADDITIVE ARCHIVE
North star: How does formal individual political agency operate inside institutions and information systems that select choices, convert votes into power, constrain dissent, measure citizens, and increasingly adapt to their behaviour?

The research programme separates historical fact, legal rules, measured effects, interpretation, and open hypotheses. Claims are graded rather than promoted because they make a better story.

Core research programme

Political architecture

Founding design; who counted as “the people”; how votes become power; participation and abstention; liberty versus majority rule; the political class; and unequal influence.

Influence machinery

Human motivation; Lippmann versus Dewey; the technology of influence from print to digital systems; polling, focus groups, voter files, experimentation, microtargeting, and Canadian political data.

Feeds and perception

Social proof, cascades, missing denominators, exemplar effects, ranking, user choice, algorithmic selection, and reciprocal feedback between people and platforms.

Conceptual boundaries

Information, persuasion, framing, targeting, optimization, nudging, manipulation, deception, propaganda, and coercion are treated as distinct concepts rather than one moral bucket.

Modern stress tests

Trump as a stress test rather than an origin story; Canadian reactions to U.S. politics; rational external threats versus source-label contamination.

Empirical gauntlet

Source Contamination, Missing Denominator, Social Proof/Cascade, Moral Emergency Override, Correction Order, and Personalized Selection/Ranking are hypotheses to be killed or supported by preregistered tests.

Eight ways to make yourself harder to bullshit

These are the book's practical checks, preserved here as the bridge from narrative to method.

1 · Swap the JerseysWould I judge the same behaviour the same way if the party names were reversed?
2 · Remove the NameWhat do I think of the policy before I know which politician proposed it?
3 · Show Me the Fucking DenominatorOut of how many? Over what period? Compared with what?
4 · Who Picked This for Me?What selection process put this example, clip, story, or claim in front of me?
5 · Find the Primary ReceiptCan I reach the statute, judgment, transcript, study, original video, dataset, archived page, or official record?
6 · Do the Archive PassFor deletion or scrubbing claims, check historical URLs, archived pages, PDFs, mirrors, contemporaneous references, and roster snapshots.
7 · What Would Change My Mind?If the honest answer is “nothing,” this is probably identity, not inquiry.
8 · Put the Bastard in the ChairWould I still support this power if the politician I distrust most controlled it tomorrow?

Research windows already developed

Franchise archaeology — U.S. + Canada

Expansion, contraction, exclusion, access, equality of voting power, and independent administration.

Rule-Scope Problem

Authority, process, scope, and liberty are separate questions; majority rule is a decision rule inside a justified jurisdiction, not unlimited subject-matter authority.

Political caste / intermediaries

Parties, consultants, bureaucracy, lobbying, elite influence, pluralist counterweights, and the limits of the “caste” metaphor.

Human motivations

Self-interest is tested against reciprocity, fairness, costly punishment, identity, status, altruism, moral conviction, and context-sensitive cooperation.

Incentive Machine

An adaptive-system explanation for polarization and attention competition that does not require a central conspiracy.

Lippmann versus Dewey

Political knowledge, rational ignorance, heuristics, party cues, misinformation, expertise, and whether low-information citizens can still make competent judgments.

Technology of influence

Pamphlets through smartphones: what each medium enabled, what friction it removed, what vulnerability it created, and which claims about its power were overstated.

The measured voter

Polling, focus groups, direct mail, voter files, scores, A/B tests, consumer matching, microtargeting, and the gap between prediction and demonstrated persuasion.

Canada political data

Elector lists, party databases, commercial and inferred data, privacy policies, current federal safeguards, oversight, and unresolved rights gaps.

Social proof + missing denominator

Visible reactions can affect behaviour; repeated authentic outliers can also distort perceived prevalence when base rates and selection processes are hidden. Both effects have important nulls and moderators.

Influence definitions

An operational decision tree separates persuasion from manipulation, deception, propaganda, coercion, targeting, optimization, framing, and nudging.

Canada as natural comparison

Canadian reactions to U.S. politics are decomposed into real economic/security consequences and possible associative source contamination.

Empirical Gauntlet

A kill-gated study ladder uses smallest effects of interest, equivalence tests, preregistration, mirror conditions, replication, and open materials rather than significance hunting.

Claim discipline

Every important claim should eventually land in one of five states: ESTABLISHED, STRONG, PLAUSIBLE, SPECULATIVE, or REJECTED. The archive will keep corrections and killed claims instead of silently rewriting history.
