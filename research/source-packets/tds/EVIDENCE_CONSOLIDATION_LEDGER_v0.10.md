### Ledger rules
Every claim must have:
- Claim ID
- Publishable sentence
- Status: ESTABLISHED / STRONG / PLAUSIBLE / SPECULATIVE / REJECTED
- Direct / primary source
- Best scholarly / secondary source
- Strongest counterevidence or boundary condition
- Effect size / denominator where relevant
- Jurisdiction
- CANNOT CLAIM

A non-significant statistical result does **not** establish absence. Null claims require adequate power and preregistered equivalence bounds.

## A. DEMOCRATIC ARCHITECTURE

### EC-001 — Mediated popular power
**Publishable sentence:** Representative democracy does not transmit a single unmediated “popular will” directly into government; individual political choices are filtered through franchise rules, representation, parties, electoral institutions and administrative procedures.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED  
**Direct / primary source:** U.S./Canadian constitutional and electoral rules; source packet Window A/B.  
**Best secondary source:** Bernard Manin, *The Principles of Representative Government*; comparative electoral-system scholarship.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Mediation does not imply fraud or absence of genuine popular influence.  
**Effect / denominator:** N/A.  
**Jurisdiction:** General representative democracies; specific mechanisms differ by country.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** “Democracy is fake.”  
**Chapter:** Foundation — What exactly are we calling democracy?  
**Audit:** YELLOW — direct source bundle still to be pinned line-by-line.

### EC-002 — Franchise was constructed and repeatedly redesigned
**Publishable sentence:** In both the United States and Canada, the electorate was repeatedly expanded, contracted and institutionally redesigned rather than moving in a simple straight line toward universal participation.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED  
**Direct / primary source:** Voting statutes, constitutional amendments, Canada Elections Act history; Window B source packet.  
**Best secondary source:** Alexander Keyssar; Elections Canada historical synthesis.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Long-run expansion of voting rights remains real despite reversals.  
**Effect / denominator:** Historical electorate sizes where used must include denominator.  
**Jurisdiction:** U.S. + Canada.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** “More voters caused democratic decline.”  
**Chapter:** Who counted as “the people”?  
**Audit:** YELLOW.

### EC-003 — Majority procedure does not create unlimited jurisdiction
**Publishable sentence:** Majority rule is a decision procedure within a political jurisdiction; by itself it does not establish that every subject is legitimately open to majority control.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED as an analytic distinction  
**Direct / primary source:** Locke, Madison, Tocqueville, Mill, Kant, Rawls and constitutional rights traditions.  
**Best secondary source:** Rule-Scope research packet.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** The legitimate boundary of collective jurisdiction is deeply contested.  
**Effect / denominator:** N/A.  
**Jurisdiction:** Liberal-democratic theory.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** “Any law supported by 51% is morally legitimate.”  
**Chapter:** The Outvoted Person / Authority → Process → Scope → Liberty.  
**Audit:** GREEN conceptually; primary quotation audit still needed for prose.

### EC-004 — Large-scale politics generates specialization
**Publishable sentence:** Large representative systems generate specialized political intermediaries—parties, staff, bureaucracies, consultants, lobbyists and experts—because continuous political administration exceeds what ordinary citizens can perform directly.  
**Status:** STRONG  
**Direct / primary source:** Party/bureaucratic institutional records.  
**Best secondary source:** Michels, Schumpeter, Dahl, Weber, modern party-organization literature.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Specialization does not prove a closed oligarchy; elections, competing elites, insurgencies and reforms can disrupt insiders.  
**Effect / denominator:** N/A.  
**Jurisdiction:** Large representative democracies.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** “A closed political caste controls everything.”  
**Chapter:** The Political Class.  
**Audit:** YELLOW.

### EC-005 — Human motives are mixed
**Publishable sentence:** Human political and social behaviour cannot be reduced to material self-interest; reciprocity, fairness, identity, status, moral conviction, altruism and punishment of norm violators also shape behaviour.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED  
**Direct / primary source:** Experimental public-goods, ultimatum and costly-punishment studies in source packet.  
**Best secondary source:** Behavioural economics / cooperation literature; Ostrom as institutional extension.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Self-interest remains important and often grows under anonymity, one-shot interaction or weak accountability.  
**Effect / denominator:** Roughly half conditional cooperators in some classic public-goods paradigms; exact numbers require direct-paper audit before publication.  
**Jurisdiction:** Cross-cultural behavioural research, not all political contexts equally.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** “Humans only seek advantage.”  
**Chapter:** Everybody Wants / Incentive Machine.  
**Audit:** YELLOW.

**Publishable sentence:** Citizens cannot directly observe most national political reality and therefore form judgments using mediated information, heuristics, institutions and trusted intermediaries.

### EC-010 — Microtargeting is more clearly a delivery system than a mind-control system

### EC-011 — Social proof is more reliable as a behavioural cue than a truth machine
**Publishable sentence:** Visible popularity signals can influence selection, engagement and perceived popularity, while their effects on whether people judge a claim to be true are mixed and often modest.

**Audit:** YELLOW.

# WORKING FREEZE

The following conceptual architecture is frozen unless contradicted by new evidence:

**Authority → Process → Scope → Liberty**

**Membership → Participation → Choice Construction → Information → Interpretation → Measurement → Targeting → Feedback → Conversion → Scope**

**Event → Record → Selection → Framing → Ranking → Exposure → Social Signals → Interpretation → Reaction → Behavioural Data → Adaptation**

**Citizen model:** an agent inside a feedback environment — neither an untouched sovereign mind nor a helpless puppet.

**North star:**  
> How does formal individual political agency operate inside institutions and information systems that construct choices, filter reality, measure response, target attention, convert votes into power, and increasingly adapt to human behaviour?

**Human ending:**  
> What do I actually mean when I say I made up my own mind?

# K. VANISHING PAUSE / SMARTPHONE ATTENTION CLOSEOUT

### EC-076 — Actual smartphone use and delay discounting
**Publishable sentence:** In observational research using actual phone-use data, heavier smartphone use is associated with steeper delay discounting and lower self-control, with similar relationships for social-media and gaming use.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED ASSOCIATION  
**Direct source:** Schulz van Endert & Mohr, *PLOS ONE* (2020), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0241383.  
**Effect / denominator:** n=101; net screen time vs larger-delayed-reward choices r≈−0.25; net screen time vs log discount parameter r≈0.21; self-control r≈−0.32.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Observational design cannot determine whether heavy use causes impatience or people high in delay discounting use smartphones more heavily.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** smartphones caused a population-wide loss of delayed gratification.  
**Chapter:** The Vanishing Pause.  
**Audit:** GREEN.

### EC-077 — Delay-discounting causality unresolved
**Publishable sentence:** Existing experimental evidence does not cleanly establish that smartphone exposure itself causes a durable increase in delay discounting.  
**Status:** STRONG  
**Evidence:** Later reviews describe early smartphone-exposure evidence inconsistently; Hadar et al.'s directly published intervention clearly reports causal changes in other cognitive/social outcomes but does not provide a secure foundation for the broad delay-discounting claim.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** phones trained a generation to prefer immediate rewards.  
**Chapter:** The Vanishing Pause / Myths We Killed.  
**Audit:** GREEN.

### EC-078 — Notification scheduling changes interruption experience
**Publishable sentence:** A randomized two-week field experiment found that batching smartphone notifications three times daily reduced reported interruption and stress and improved reported attention, productivity, mood and control, whereas eliminating notifications entirely increased anxiety and fear of missing out.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED in tested design  
**Direct source:** Fitz et al., *Computers in Human Behavior* 101 (2019), DOI 10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016.  
**Effect / denominator:** n=237.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Many outcomes were self-reported; hourly batching produced little change.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** turning off every notification necessarily improves attention or well-being.  
**Chapter:** The Vanishing Pause.  
**Audit:** GREEN.

### EC-079 — Blocking mobile internet can improve sustained attention
**Publishable sentence:** A preregistered randomized trial found that blocking mobile-internet access on smartphones for two weeks produced a modest improvement in objectively measured sustained attention.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED  
**Direct source:** Castelo et al., *PNAS Nexus* (2025), DOI 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf017.  
**Effect / denominator:** 467 initially agreed; pooled ITT pre/post sustained-attention effect d_z≈0.23; delayed-treatment replication d_z≈0.28.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Compliance was difficult: 266 set up the block and 119 met strict preregistered compliance; the intervention blocked all mobile internet rather than identifying a single app/mechanism.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** smartphones cause permanent cognitive damage, or social media specifically caused the effect.  
**Chapter:** The Vanishing Pause.  
**Audit:** GREEN.

### EC-080 — Disordered screen use and cognition
**Publishable sentence:** Cross-sectional meta-analysis finds that people classified with disordered screen-use behaviours perform worse on average on attention and executive-function measures.  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED ASSOCIATION  
**Direct source:** Moshel et al., *Neuropsychology Review* 34 (2024), DOI 10.1007/s11065-023-09612-4.  
**Effect / denominator:** 34 meta-analysed studies; 1,076 disordered-use participants + 1,338 controls; overall g≈0.38; attention g≈0.50; executive function g≈0.31; high heterogeneity.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Cross-sectional; samples skew young/Asian/male; “disordered” use is not ordinary smartphone ownership.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** normal smartphone use causes the same deficits.  
**Chapter:** The Vanishing Pause.  
**Audit:** GREEN.

### EC-081 — Mere-presence “brain drain” is limited
**Publishable sentence:** A meta-analysis of 56 studies found only limited evidence that the mere presence of a smartphone broadly impairs cognition, with a significant pooled effect for working memory but null pooled effects for the other examined cognitive domains.  
**Status:** STRONG  
**Direct source:** Parry, *Media Psychology* 27(5) (2024), DOI 10.1080/15213269.2023.2286647.  
**Effect / denominator:** k=56; n=7,093.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** Methodological heterogeneity and generally low statistical power limit certainty.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** a nearby phone universally drains cognitive ability.  
**Chapter:** The Vanishing Pause / Myths We Killed.  
**Audit:** GREEN.

### EC-082 — The Vanishing Pause hypothesis
**Publishable sentence:** Smartphones have reduced access, interruption, stopping and response friction; some experiments show that restoring portions of that friction can improve attention-related outcomes, but the downstream effect on political judgment and critical thinking remains unestablished.  
**Status:** PLAUSIBLE SYNTHESIS  
**Evidence:** notification RCT; mobile-internet-block RCT; task-switching literature; observational delay-discounting findings.  
**Counterevidence / boundary:** no direct experiment currently demonstrates that added smartphone friction improves political reasoning or reduces polarization.  
**CANNOT CLAIM:** the Vanishing Pause is a proven causal mechanism of democratic decline.  
**Chapter:** The Vanishing Pause / Why Am I in a Hurry?  
**Audit:** YELLOW-GREEN.
